Scholar Points

I do not offer extra credit. Extra credit works from the back end: something you do late in the term to repair a grade the work already produced. Scholar Points work from the front end. You earn them by putting hours into studying this material with other people while the term is running, which is the thing that was going to improve your grade anyway. The bump is the acknowledgment, not the reason.

  • Thirty hours across the term earns the full 2.5 percent. Fewer hours earn a proportion of it. Fifteen hours is 1.25 percent, six hours is half a percent. Nothing is all or nothing.
  • Three hours a week is the most that counts. Do more if you want to, and you should, but a single marathon weekend cannot stand in for showing up across the term. Spacing is the whole reason this works.
  • Hosting earns one extra hour. A two hour session you hosted logs as three. Not double, just the one hour on top, however long it ran.
  • The weekly cap applies after the host hour, so a two hour session you hosted fills the week on its own.
  • It is applied at the end of the term to your final course percentage, after everything else is in.
  • It can only help you. Skipping it leaves your grade exactly where your work put it.

What makes a session count

None of this is here to be difficult. There is a percentage riding on it, and a percentage on a grade has to rest on something I can actually see.

  • Online, not in person. Zoom, Google Meet, Discord, anything you like that records. An in person study session is still worth doing, it just cannot be verified, so it does not go on the log.
  • Record it. Whoever hosts records the session and keeps the link. That is what shows me it happened and that people were working, not just signed in.
  • Cameras on. Everybody, the whole session. A black square is not attendance.
  • Stay the whole time. Log the time you were actually present. Arriving late or leaving early is fine, log what you were there for. Do not log an hour you spent in another tab.
  • One session at a time. Sitting in two rooms at once means neither can be verified, so neither counts.
  • Hosts keep the recording and the attendance list. If you ran it, you are the one who can show who was there and for how long.

If something about this is a problem for you, cameras especially, come and talk to me rather than skipping the whole thing. There is almost always a way to sort it out.

Logging your hours

The Scholar Points page has the log. Add each session as you finish it, with the date, how long you were there, whether you hosted, and the recording link. It adds itself up and shows you where you are against the thirty hours.

There is a weekly assignment in Canvas for your hours. At the end of each week, open the Scholar Points page, press Save this week for Canvas, and upload that PDF.

Your log lives in your browser

It is stored on your own device and nowhere else, so it keeps adding up all term and you never start over. Use the same browser on the same device and it will still be there in December. If you are about to switch machines or clear your history, save a PDF first.

What to do in a session

The sessions that work are the ones where everybody is producing physiology out loud, not watching one person talk. Pick a single activity and actually do it rather than planning a syllabus for the hour.

  • Brain dump the same competency

    Everyone writes on blank paper for five minutes, then you read each other's and find what you all missed. The overlap is the real gap.

  • Teach it at a whiteboard

    One person shares a screen, or holds a real whiteboard up to the camera, and talks and draws at the same time, the way the midterm asks for. Everyone else holds the checklist and ticks what actually got covered.

  • Run a Kahoot

    Somebody builds a quick one on this week's competencies beforehand and you play it together. Fast, loud, and it surfaces the things nobody knew.

  • Work the book problems together

    Everyone tries the same Silverthorn problem cold, then you compare routes. Two people getting the same answer different ways teaches more than either answer.

  • Quiz from the Rx Cards

    Open Rx Cards, read one out, and let the group answer before anyone looks. Say why the wrong options are wrong, not just which one is right.

  • Trace a pathway out loud

    Pick a loop or a mechanism and hand it round, one step per person, each saying what happens next and why. Whoever stalls has found their own repair target.

The brain dump Rx Cards

A simple run of show

  1. Warm up, five minutes. Everyone names three things from last week without looking. It gets people talking before anything hard starts.
  2. Do your one thing. Spend the bulk of the time on it and take turns, so nobody hides and nobody carries the whole hour.
  3. Go back to the misses. Whatever the group got wrong is the most useful thing in the room. Re-do those before you finish.
  4. Close it out. Each person says one thing they are going to review on their own tonight, then you stop on time.

Hosting one yourself

Teaching somebody else is one of the strongest ways to learn something yourself. When you host a session you are not doing anyone a favor. You are getting the best study hour of your week, and an extra hour of Scholar Points on top of it.

  • Post it before you need it. A session announced the night before gets one person. Two or three days ahead gets a group.
  • Say what you are working on. "Week 4 membrane potential, brain dump and Kahoot" pulls people in. "Studying, anyone want to join" does not.
  • Start on time and end on time. An hour that ends when it said it would is one people come back to.
  • Small is fine. Two people quizzing each other is a real session. You are not failing if it is not eight.
  • Nobody has to be the expert. The person who understands it least often asks the question that fixes it for everyone.
  • Use whatever records. Zoom, Google Meet, Discord. It has to be online, because the recording is what makes the hour count for everybody who turned up.
  • Start the recording before you start talking. It is the one step people forget, and without it the hour does not count for anybody.

Getting it on the calendar. Send me the day, the time, what you are working on and your join link, and it goes up at the top of this page where everybody in the course will see it. That is the whole process.

The one line that matters

This is not where graded work gets done together. Your lab write-up, your patient chart entry and your discussion posts are yours, done by you, in your own words.

Practicing the same competency in a group and then going away and writing your own answer is exactly right. Sitting in a call filling in the same worksheet together is not.

Everything on this page is practice. None of it is a place to produce something you turn in.

Questions people ask

Do I have to go?

No. Plenty of people study better alone and there is nothing wrong with that. If that is you, skip this page and your grade is exactly what your work put it at. Scholar Points can only add.

Why do we have to record it?

Because a percentage on your grade has to rest on something I can see. The recording is how I know the session happened and that people were there start to finish. I am not watching them for content and I am not grading anything in them.

Can we work on the lab or the patient chart together?

You can talk about the physiology behind them as much as you like. What you turn in has to be yours, written by you, in your own words.

What if I host and nobody comes?

Then you got an hour of quiet study you had already committed to, which is not a bad outcome. Post it again the following week. The first few are always small.

What if I forget to log a session?

Add it when you remember, with the date it actually happened. The log sorts by date and the weekly cap is worked out from the dates, not from the order you typed them in.

Which app should we use?

Whichever one you like, as long as it records. Zoom, Google Meet and Discord all do. Zoom is the one most people already have through the college, so it is the usual answer, but nothing here requires it.

Can we meet in person instead?

Not for Scholar Points. Sessions run online so they can be recorded, and the recording is what lets an hour count. Studying with somebody at a table is still a good use of your time, it just is not something I can verify, so it does not go on the log.
