How grading works.

Four categories, a scale, and a short list of things that are deliberately not graded.

The four categories

Each category is named for what it asks you to do, not for the format it arrives in. The work that carries the most weight is the work that proves the reasoning is yours.

Show Me What You Know, 35 percent

Two exams, 17.5 percent each. They are not multiple choice. You draw a physiological pathway and teach it out loud on video, with no notes.

Each exam is a three day window, not an hour.

Midterm 1 covers Weeks 1 to 7. Window October 26 to 28. Midterm 2 covers Weeks 8 to 14. Window December 14 to 16.

Investigate It, 25 percent

The weekly labs. You generate and interpret real physiological output, then write it up in the same structure a clinical write up uses: question, prediction, evidence, interpretation, conclusion.

Where a week uses PhysioEx, it takes both halves. PhysioEx must show complete in Pearson, and the points live on your worksheet.

Use It, 25 percent

Weekly application case, 20 percent

Each week you choose one case from three or four set in different rooms, and turn it in on Sunday. They differ in context, not in rigor. Every one of them assesses the same underlying competency.

Patient chart, 5 percent

This is the capstone. You keep it by hand all term, adding each week's data and your thinking about it. Nothing is collected week to week. The finished chart is turned in once and graded as one piece. Exactly what you upload.

The patient chart is due Wednesday, December 16.

Think About It, 15 percent

One discussion post a week, carrying the physiology and your thinking about it together. Decision, evidence, adjustment.

Initial post Friday at 10:00 pm, so there is something to reply to. Replies Sunday at 10:00 pm.

What is not graded, and why that is deliberate

Several things you do every week carry no points at all.

  • The note sheet and the retrieval work. This is where you find out what you do not know. Grading it would push you to make it look finished instead of honest, and an honest sheet with gaps in it is worth more to you than a tidy one.
  • The practice items. Predict, commit, check, correct, explain. Getting these wrong is the point of doing them.
  • The book problems. There are two ways to use them, and both count as learning. Work them forwards if you have a way in. If you open one and have no idea how to start, read the worked solution and write beside each line why that step is there, then reproduce it from blank paper.
  • The Mastery Check. No points and no penalty, and you can take it as many times as you want. It does score you, and it does more than that. It reports competency by competency, separates what you got wrong while feeling sure from what you got wrong while unsure, and tells you what to go back to. None of those numbers reach the gradebook.
  • Spaced recall. Week 3 has to still be there in October, and spacing is what does that.

None of this is optional in any way that matters. It is the whole route to the four categories above. The students who skip it are the ones who arrive at a midterm able to recognize a pathway and unable to draw one.
