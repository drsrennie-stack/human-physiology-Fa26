Part 2. Grip fatigue, at home

Squeeze until your hand tires

PhysioEx shows fatigue in an isolated muscle. This part shows it in yours. There is no surface EMG at home, so you count instead, and Part 2 asks what an EMG would have shown.

  1. Hold the clothespin between the tip of your thumb and the tip of your index finger of the hand you write with. Rest your forearm on a table.
  2. Start the timer. Squeeze the clothespin all the way closed and let it open, as fast as you can, for 2 minutes without stopping.
  3. Count the squeezes in every 15 seconds, or have your partner count while you squeeze, and write each count in the table.
  4. Put the clothespin down and rest for 2 minutes. Then count one more 15 seconds.

A burning feeling in your forearm is expected and goes away with rest. Stop if you feel sharp pain, and skip this part if you have a hand or wrist injury. Say so on your worksheet instead.

Part 3. Clinical correlation

Sore on Wednesday, not on Monday

An athlete does a session of downhill running on Monday and feels fine that evening. Soreness begins Tuesday afternoon, peaks Wednesday and is gone by Friday. Blood lactate returns to its resting level within about an hour of any hard session. Downhill running loads the thigh muscles while they are lengthening.
