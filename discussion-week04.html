Week 4, a practice run

Week 4 discussion: practice the exam format.

First post due Friday, October 2 at 10:00 pm. Two replies due Sunday, October 4 at 10:00 pm.

This week's discussion is a practice run of your exam format, done on cell transport from Week 2. The material is familiar, so you can put your attention on the format itself: brain dumps from memory, then one hard multiple choice question you answer, model, teach, and check against a rubric. Doing it once now means the exam will not be the first time.

How to do it

  1. Close your notes, the slides, and the book for Parts 1 and 2. The point is to find out what you can do from memory.
  2. Give each brain dump about 10 minutes. Write or draw everything you can, then stop.
  3. Only open a rubric after you finish that part. Check honestly: a point counts only if you clearly said it.

Part 1: Two brain dumps

Brain dump 1: the pump that powers another transporter

Trace how the Na+/K+ ATPase powers calcium removal by the Na+/Ca2+ exchanger in a heart muscle cell. Then predict what happens to intracellular Ca2+ and to contraction strength if the pump is partly inhibited, and explain why.

Open the rubric after you finish

Brain dump 2: glucose from the gut to the blood

Trace one glucose molecule from the lumen of the small intestine to the blood. Name the transporter at each membrane, say which membrane it sits on, state whether each step is uphill or downhill for glucose, and explain why the Na+/K+ ATPase is essential even though it never touches glucose.

Open the rubric after you finish

Part 2: The hard question

Ouabain is added to an intestinal epithelial cell. Trace the effect on intracellular Na+ and then on glucose uptake through SGLT at the apical membrane.

Choose your answer before you do anything else.

Design your model

Draw a model that answers the question: an intestinal cell with its apical side facing the lumen and its basolateral side facing the blood. Put the Na+/K+ ATPase and SGLT on the correct membranes. Use arrows to show where Na+ and glucose move normally, then mark what changes when ouabain is added. Photograph your drawing for your post.

Teach it

Record a teaching demo of 2 to 3 minutes. Using your model, teach the answer as if to a classmate who missed class: say why the right answer is right, then explain why each of the other three answers is wrong. A phone video is fine.

Check your teaching against the rubric

Watch your video once, then check each point you clearly made. The correct answer is in the first line, so do this only after you have recorded.

Open the teaching rubric after you record
