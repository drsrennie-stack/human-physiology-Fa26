Case 1 · Capillary exchange and edema

The swollen ankles

What you know

A 34-year-old has had puffy eyes in the morning and swollen ankles for three weeks. Weight is up 4 kg (8.8 lb). Blood pressure 128/80 mmHg. The kidneys are losing protein: urine protein is 6 g a day (normal is under 0.15 g a day). Serum albumin is 1.9 g/dL (reference 3.5 to 5.0).

Your decision

Compare giving albumin through an IV, a diuretic with less salt in the diet, a drug that lowers the protein lost in the urine (an ACE inhibitor), and raising the legs with compression stockings. For each one, say how long its effect would last in someone whose kidneys are leaking protein, and why. Choose an approach or a sequence.

Your drawing must show

A capillary with its arterial and venous ends, the blood pressure inside the capillary pushing fluid out and the plasma proteins, mostly albumin, pulling it back in, the net movement of fluid at each end, the lymph vessel, and where your intervention acts.

Challenge question

Lymph flow goes up when more fluid leaves the capillaries. Why does edema form anyway?

02

Case 2 · Blood pressure control in blood loss

The fall from the bike

What you know

A 24-year-old who weighs 60 kg (132 lb) crashed a bike and broke a thigh bone. About 1.2 L of blood is lost into the thigh, close to 30 percent of the blood volume (about 4.2 L for 60 kg). Heart rate 118 beats/min (normal 60 to 100). Blood pressure 112/88 mmHg. Breathing 24 a minute (normal 12 to 20). Skin is pale and cool. Urine output is 25 mL an hour (normal is at least 30 mL an hour for this weight). Alert but anxious.

Your decision

Compare IV fluid (saline), a blood transfusion, a drug that constricts blood vessels, and splinting the leg, then surgery to repair the bone and the torn vessels. Choose an approach or a sequence. Explain what each one does to venous return, cardiac output and total peripheral resistance.

Your drawing must show

MAP = cardiac output × total peripheral resistance, venous return, the baroreceptor reflex, the kidney and the hormones that control it (renin from the kidney, angiotensin II, aldosterone from the adrenal cortex, ADH from the posterior pituitary), and where your intervention acts.

Challenge question

This person's blood pressure is close to normal. Why are they still in danger, and which numbers show it?
