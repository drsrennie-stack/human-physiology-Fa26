Online classes get lonely, and this is the fix

An online class can be an isolating way to learn something hard, and physiology is hard. You are reading on your own, drawing on your own, and sitting an exam on your own, and it is easy to go a whole term without ever hearing another student say any of this out loud.

Scholar Points exist to give you a reason to fix that. If your schedule allows it and you would like to, you can work through this material with your classmates, teach each other, quiz each other, and find out that the thing you thought only you found confusing is confusing to everybody.

It is optional, not required. Nothing here is held against you if you never do it. But it is highly recommended, because the students who talk this material through with somebody tend to do better, and they tend to enjoy the term more.

What the hours are worth

Thirty hours across the term earns the full 2.5 percent. Fewer hours earn the same share of it, at the same rate. Nothing here is all or nothing, so six hours is worth doing and so is sixteen.

What qualifies

Five things. All five have to be true for a session to count.

  1. It is posted on the Study With Me page and open to everyone. This is the one people miss. A private session with your two friends does not count. If you want to host, you post it so any student in the class can see it and join. Closed groups are welcome to exist, they just do not earn points.
  2. People sign up. Sessions are signed up for on the Study With Me page, so the host knows who is coming and there is a record of who was there.
  3. It happens online and it is recorded. Zoom, Google Meet, Discord, anything that records. Whoever hosts records it and keeps the link.
  4. Everybody is on camera, the whole time. All of you, for the whole session. A room of black squares is not what this is for and I cannot count it.
  5. You log what you were actually there for. Arriving late or leaving early is fine. Log the time you were present and working, not the time the meeting was open.

If any of this is a problem for you, cameras especially, come and talk to me instead of skipping the whole thing. There is almost always a way to sort it out.

What you can actually do together

Anything where you are producing this material out loud with other people. Some things that work well:

  • Play a learning game. Run a Kahoot, quiz each other on competencies, race through the structures. Games are on the list because they work, not as a consolation prize.
  • Work the book problems together. Everybody tries the problem first, then you compare what you got and argue about it.
  • Do brain dumps side by side. Same prompt, everyone draws or writes it from memory with nothing open, then you hold them up and see what each person left out.
  • Teach a competency. One person explains a mechanism on camera with no notes while everybody else asks the awkward questions.
  • Go through Rx Cards together and say the answers out loud before you flip them.

What does not count: time you spent studying alone, and doing graded work together, which is not allowed anyway. Your labs, chart entries and Mastery Checks are yours.

If you host, you get an extra hour

  • Hosting adds one hour to your log, whatever the length of the session. A two hour session you hosted logs as three. It is one extra hour, not double.
  • Why: preparing a session is real work that happens before anyone shows up. Building the Kahoot, picking the competency, keeping four people on track.
  • The three hour weekly cap is applied after the host hour. So a two hour session you hosted fills that week on its own.
  • Hosts keep the recording and the sign-up list. If you ran it, you are the one who can show who was there and for how long.

Logging and turning it in

Keep your own log and write each session down right after it ends, while you still remember who was there. Every entry needs:

  • The date
  • Whether you hosted or attended
  • Hours, to the quarter hour. Twenty minutes is 0.33 hours
  • What you worked on, for example the week and the competency
  • Who was there. First names are fine
  • The recording link
  • Your confirmation that cameras were on and you were there for the time you logged

Turn your hours in through Canvas at the end of each week, with your running total, so we both know where you are. At the end of the term send me the whole log and I apply the bump.

Weeks 2 and 3 are one block. Submit those hours once, by Sunday, September 27. The three hour cap still counts week by week inside the block, so up to six hours can count across the two weeks. Week 4 opens September 28 and goes back to one submission a week.

Why this is not extra credit

I do not offer extra credit. Extra credit is a fix bolted on at the end of a term so a grade that went badly can be pulled up, usually with work that has little to do with the course. It rewards whoever is furthest behind and it teaches nobody anything.

Scholar Points work from the other end. They are not a rescue. They are what happens when you put hours into the practice that makes this course easier while you are still taking it. Students who do that tend to have a better grade before any bump is applied, and the bump goes on top of that.

Which is the honest pitch. The 2.5 percent is real and I will apply it, but it is the smaller half of what you get. The larger half is that the exams get easier, because you spent the term saying this material out loud instead of rereading it.

Questions people ask

Is 2.5 percent enough to change my letter grade?

Sometimes, at a boundary. An 87.5 becomes a 90. It is not enough to rescue a grade that is well down, and it was never meant to be, which is the difference between this and extra credit.

What if I can only manage ten hours?

Then you earn about 0.83 percent, and you also spent ten hours practicing physiology out loud. Both are worth having. There is no threshold you have to clear before anything counts.

Can I log time I studied alone?

No. Studying alone is most of what this course asks of you and it already shows up in your grade through the work itself. This is for the hours you spend working it with other people, because that is the part almost nobody does unless something nudges them.

Can I just study with my roommate and log it?

Not for points. Study with your roommate all you like, it is good for you. To count, a session has to be posted on the Study With Me page where anyone in the class can sign up for it. That is what keeps this from turning into a private club.

What if I post a session and nobody comes?

Post it again for another time and tell me. If a session is not getting takers it is usually the time slot, not you, and I can help you find a better one or put the word out.

What if somebody cannot have their camera on?

Come and talk to me. There are real reasons, and none of them should cost you the hours. What I cannot do is take a room full of black squares as evidence that a session happened.

Do you check?

I read the logs. I am not auditing timestamps. An honor system that somebody games mostly cheats them out of the hours, which were the actual point. Put down what you did.

What if I host and two people come for twenty minutes?

Log the time you were there working. Twenty minutes is 0.33 hours, plus your host hour. Small sessions are real sessions.
