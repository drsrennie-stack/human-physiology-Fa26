Week 5, before the first pass

Week 5 pre-read.

About 30 minutes. Graded complete or incomplete. Do this before the first pass.

This is a quick look ahead, not a full read. Look at the headings and the figures, answer the questions below in a few words, and move on. You will learn all of it properly in the walkthroughs, and it will make much more sense because you have already seen it once. This week covers three chapters: Chapter 14 (reflexes), Chapter 10 (the spinal cord, cerebrospinal fluid and the blood-brain barrier), and Chapter 11 (sensory physiology). Every section below has a part for each.

Where to look

Read closely, Silverthorn Chapter 14, Integrative Physiology I: Control of Body Movement: only the part on neural reflexes and skeletal muscle reflexes. That covers how reflexes are sorted, the muscle spindle, the Golgi tendon organ, and the withdrawal and crossed extensor reflexes. The rest of Chapter 14 is next week.

Read closely, Silverthorn Chapter 10, The Central Nervous System: the parts on the membranes, cerebrospinal fluid and the blood-brain barrier, and the whole section on the spinal cord. In the section on the brain, read the part on the brain stem and look over Table 10.1, the cranial nerves, which you use in lab. In the section on brain function, read only the parts on how the spinal cord and brain handle sensory information and on motor output. The rest of the chapter, on sleep, memory, language and emotion, is not part of this week.

Then, Silverthorn Chapter 11, Sensory Physiology. Read the first part, on the general properties of sensory systems, and the part on the somatic senses, including pain. For the special senses, vision, hearing, balance, taste and smell, only skim: look at the figures of the eye and the ear and read the chapter's Visual Summary. The walkthroughs teach what you need from them.

Chapter numbers are for the 9th edition. Other editions number things differently, so go by the chapter title and the topic, and use the search in your eText to find it.

Figures to look at closely

These are the most important figures for this week. Open each one in your eText, read its labels, and look for the thing named after it.

Chapter 14, reflexes

  • Table 14.1, Classification of Neural Reflexes. The four ways a reflex can be sorted.
  • Fig. 14.1, Neural Reflexes. Count the synapses in each of the three reflexes.
  • Fig. 14.2, Muscle Spindles and Alpha-gamma Coactivation. Find which part of the spindle has no myofibrils, and where the gamma motor neuron attaches.
  • Fig. 14.3, Golgi tendon organs. Find what squeezes the nerve endings when the muscle contracts.
  • Fig. 14.5, The patellar tendon (knee jerk) reflex. Follow both efferent paths: one to the quadriceps and one to the hamstrings.
  • Fig. 14.6, The crossed extensor reflex. Find which muscles contract and which are inhibited in each leg.

Chapter 10, the spinal cord and protecting the brain

  • Fig. 10.6, Organization of the spinal cord. Find the posterior horn, the anterior horn, and where the ascending and descending tracts run.
  • Fig. 10.4, Cerebrospinal Fluid. Trace the fluid from where it is made to where it goes back into the blood.
  • Fig. 10.5, The blood-brain barrier. Find the two kinds of cells that help keep the capillary tight.
  • Table 10.1, The Cranial Nerves. You will use this in lab.

Chapter 11, sensory physiology

  • Fig. 11.2, Receptive fields. See why two points close together are felt as one on the arm but as two on a fingertip.
  • Fig. 11.7, Receptor adaptation. Compare the action potentials from a tonic and a phasic receptor.
  • Fig. 11.8, Somatosensory pathways. Find where pain crosses the midline and where fine touch crosses.
  • Fig. 11.10, Sensory receptors in the skin. Find the receptor that senses steady pressure and texture.
  • Fig. 11.11, Referred pain. Find where pain from the heart is felt.
  • For the special senses, just look at: Fig. 11.24 (the eye), Fig. 11.26 (optics of the eye), Fig. 11.18 (signal transduction in hair cells) and Fig. 11.21 (equilibrium).

What this reading should help you do

Read each goal and pick the answer that fits you right now. There is no wrong answer here.

Reflexes and the spinal cord (Chapters 14 and 10)

Name the five parts of a reflex arc, and sort a reflex as somatic or autonomic, spinal or cranial, innate or learned, and monosynaptic or polysynaptic.
Explain what the muscle spindle and the Golgi tendon organ each detect, and what their reflexes do.
Trace the withdrawal and crossed extensor reflexes, and explain why they need interneurons.
Tell an upper motor neuron problem from a lower motor neuron problem by the reflexes and muscle tone.
Describe what the gray matter and white matter of the cord hold, which pathways carry touch, pain and movement, and where cerebrospinal fluid and the blood-brain barrier fit in.

Sensory physiology (Chapter 11)

Explain how a receptor turns a stimulus into a signal, and how the brain knows what kind of stimulus it was, where, and how strong.
Explain how pain is carried and how it can be turned down.
Name what each special sense detects, and the main structures of the eye and the ear.
