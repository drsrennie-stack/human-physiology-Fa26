Camila Reyes, 19

Second year kinesiology student and a midfielder on the college soccer team. No prior medical history, no medications. You have followed her since her preseason physical in Week 1.

What this is

Every week this term you opened that week's case, wrote the new numbers into your paper chart, added to her problem list, drew the control loop, and answered the week's questions. None of that was collected. This is where it all comes in, as one document, and it is graded as one piece.

The weekly Use It cases you turned in each Sunday were graded on that week's reasoning. The chart is graded on the trend: whether you can read fifteen columns side by side and tell her whole story, and whether your thinking about her changed as the evidence did. Say when you changed your mind. A chart that was never wrong scores lower than one that was wrong in Week 4, noticed it in Week 7, and said so.

Exactly what you upload

One PDF, in this order

  1. Face sheet. Her name, your name, your entry point (the same one you used all term), and the Week 1 baseline.
  2. Vitals flowsheet, both pages, all fifteen columns filled in with units.
  3. Lab flowsheet, both pages, all fifteen columns, units written the first time each test appears.
  4. Problem list, numbered, with the week each problem was found, the numbers that were your evidence, and the week it resolved if it did.
  5. Medication and intervention log.
  6. The fifteen week pages, Week 1 through Week 15, each one with the control loop drawn, the five questions answered, and your entry point question answered.
  7. Your AI disclosure on the last page, even if it is one line saying you used none.

That is about twenty pages. Everything on it is handwritten, on the chart you printed in Week 1. If you lost a page along the way, print that page again from the chart and fill it in; do not type it.

How to make the PDF

  1. Put the pages in the order above and check that every page has your name on it. The chart prints your name line on every page; fill it in if you have not.
  2. Scan or photograph every page in good light, straight on, one page per image. A phone scanning app (Notes on iPhone, Google Drive or Adobe Scan on Android) will make one PDF from all of them.
  3. Open the PDF and page through it once. Every number on the flowsheets must be readable. If a column is blurry, rescan that page. I cannot grade what I cannot read, and a blurry column counts as a blank one.
  4. Name the file LastName_FirstName_PatientChart.pdf.
  5. Upload it here and wait for the Canvas confirmation screen. One file. If you upload twice, the later one is the one I grade.

What a strong chart looks like

  • The flowsheets are complete. Fifteen columns, every value that week gave you, with units. A trend you cannot see is a trend you cannot reason about.
  • The problem list grew and changed. Problems appear the week you could name them, with the numbers that made you name them, and some of them resolve.
  • Each loop names its parts. The regulated variable, the sensor, the integrating center, the effector, and the direction of the response, drawn for that week's physiology, in your hand.
  • The week pages read as one person thinking about one patient. Week 9 refers back to Week 3. Week 15 reads September 22 again with everything you learned after it.
  • Your thinking changed where the evidence did, and you said so on the page.

A correct number with no explanation earns half credit. That was true every week, and it is true here.

Using AI on this one

Open, with disclosure, the same as the weekly cases. Use it to check a mechanism or push back on your own reasoning. What you upload is handwritten and it is your reading of her chart, which is very visible when it is missing. Say what you used it for on the last page. Undisclosed use is the only version of this that is a problem.

If you fall behind on the chart during the term

Nothing is collected week to week, which means nobody will notice a gap until December except you. Catch a missed week the following week while the case is still fresh. Do not leave fifteen weeks of flowsheets for the last weekend; the point of the chart is that you were watching her all term, and a chart filled in from memory in December reads exactly like one.
