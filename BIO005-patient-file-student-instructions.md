# The Patient File

BIO 005 Human Physiology
Dr. Sharilyn Rennie

You have a patient. You will have the same patient for the whole term. Nobody will tell you what is wrong with them. Your job is to find out, one week at a time, using what you learn in class that week.

## How it works

**Open the file and type your name.** Type it exactly the way it appears in Canvas, and type it the same way every single week. Your name is what picks your patient. A different spelling gives you a different patient and you will lose your thread.

**Each week opens with a code.** I post it in an announcement on Monday. Enter it and the week opens.

**Every week runs the same six steps.**

1. **Predict first.** Before you can order anything, you write what you think is going on and why. There are no results on the page yet. There is nothing to look up. This is on purpose, and it is the part I care most about.
2. **Order what you want to know.** You get five or six order tokens a week. Tests cost one, two, or three. You cannot order everything. Choose.
3. **Commit before each result appears.** When you pick a test, you say what you expect, which order you are giving up to place it, and why that trade is worth it. Then, and only then, the result appears.
4. **Say what it changed.** You cannot place another order until you write what the last result changed in your thinking. "Nothing changed" is a legitimate answer if you say why that is informative.
5. **Draw it.** Use the drawing area, or draw on paper and photograph it. Either way, describe your drawing in words in the box. The description is graded with the drawing.
6. **Answer the synthesis questions and close the week.** Then export your PDF and upload it to the week's assignment.

## What is being graded

Not whether you get the diagnosis. The reasoning trail: what you predicted before you knew, what you chose to find out and in what order, and what you did with the answer when it came back.

**A correct number with no explanation earns half credit.** Every week, every question.

Real reasoning has wrong turns in it. A file that shows you chasing the wrong idea in Week 4, noticing it in Week 7, and saying so, scores better than a file that was never wrong. Say when you changed your mind. That is the whole point.

## Three things that will save you trouble

**Export the PDF every week and upload it.** The PDF is the assignment. What sits in your browser is not a submission.

**Copy your save code and keep it.** Click "Show my save code" and paste it into a document you own. If your browser gets cleared, or you switch computers, that code brings your typed work back. It does not bring your drawings back, which is the other reason to export the PDF every week.

**Do not use a shared or public computer without saving your code first.** Campus machines and private windows wipe themselves.

## About using AI on this

You will notice that the page does not show you a result until after you have committed to what you expect. That is deliberate. At the moment your thinking actually matters there is nothing on the screen for a chatbot to read.

Use whatever you like to check a definition, or to ask how a mechanism works. What I am reading is whether your prediction, your order sequence, and your revision fit together, week over week, into one person thinking. That is not something a tool can hand you, and it is very visible when it is missing.

I want your thinking.
