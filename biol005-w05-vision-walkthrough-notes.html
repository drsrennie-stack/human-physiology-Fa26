Focusing light

  • Refraction is the bending of light. The cornea does about two thirds of it and has a fixed shape. The lens does the rest and can change shape.
  • The lens is elastic and held by zonule fibers attached to the ring-shaped ciliary muscle.

Pupillary light reflex
Light in one eye constricts both pupils. In: optic nerve. Center: midbrain. Out: parasympathetic fibers in the oculomotor nerve to the circular muscle of each iris. The response in the unlit eye is the consensual response.
Afferent defect
Light in the affected eye constricts neither pupil, light in the other eye constricts both: the problem is in the optic nerve of the affected eye.

Phototransduction

  • Rhodopsin is opsin plus retinal (from vitamin A), in the discs of the rod outer segment.
  • In the dark: cGMP is high, cGMP-gated cation channels are open, sodium enters, the rod sits near −40 mV and releases glutamate continuously.
  1. A photon is absorbed and retinal changes shape.
  2. Rhodopsin is activated.
  3. Rhodopsin activates the G protein transducin.
  4. Transducin activates phosphodiesterase.
  5. Phosphodiesterase breaks down cGMP.
  6. cGMP-gated channels close and sodium entry stops.
  7. The photoreceptor hyperpolarizes toward −70 mV.
  8. Fewer voltage-gated calcium channels at the base are open, so glutamate release falls.
  • The cascade amplifies at each step, so a rod can respond to a single photon.
  • Photoreceptors make graded potentials only. The first action potentials are in the ganglion cells, whose axons form the optic nerve.
