What this week covers

Reflexes, and sensing the world. Work through these in order. Each line is one thing you will be able to do by Sunday.

  1. Reflex arc components. Diagram a reflex arc naming the receptor, sensory neuron, integrating center, motor neuron, and effector, and classify the reflex as monosynaptic or polysynaptic.
  2. Muscle spindle and stretch reflex. Explain how the muscle spindle detects stretch and trace the stretch reflex including reciprocal inhibition of the antagonist.
  3. Golgi tendon organ. Contrast the stimulus and the reflex response of the Golgi tendon organ with those of the muscle spindle.
  4. Withdrawal and crossed extensor reflexes. Trace the flexor withdrawal reflex and the crossed extensor reflex and explain how the two together preserve balance.
  5. Ascending and descending pathways. Compare the dorsal column, spinothalamic, and corticospinal pathways by the information carried, the side of decussation, and the deficit produced by a lesion.
  6. Cerebrospinal fluid and the blood brain barrier. State the functions of cerebrospinal fluid and explain how the blood brain barrier selects what reaches neurons, including why lipid soluble drugs cross readily.
  7. Reflex testing and reaction time. Elicit and grade deep tendon reflexes and measure reaction time to distinguish a reflex response from a voluntary response by latency.
  8. Sensory transduction. Explain how a stimulus is converted into a receptor potential and how that graded potential becomes a train of action potentials in the sensory neuron.
  9. Receptor classification. Classify a receptor by stimulus type as a mechanoreceptor, chemoreceptor, thermoreceptor, photoreceptor, or nociceptor and give a body location for each.
  10. Stimulus coding. Explain how the nervous system encodes modality, location, intensity, and duration and identify the labeled line principle.
  11. Receptive fields and acuity. Relate receptive field size and lateral inhibition to two point discrimination and predict which body regions have the finest acuity.
  12. Receptor adaptation. Distinguish tonic from phasic receptors by their adaptation rate and match each to a stimulus the body needs to monitor continuously or only at onset.
  13. Somatosensory pathways. Trace touch and pain information from receptor to cortex and explain why the somatosensory homunculus is disproportionate.
  14. Pain and its modulation. Distinguish fast and slow pain by fiber type, explain referred pain by convergence, and describe how gate control and endogenous opioids reduce pain transmission.
  15. Tactile mapping and adaptation testing. Measure two point discrimination at several body sites and show receptor adaptation and referred sensation experimentally.
  16. Optics and accommodation. Explain refraction by the cornea and lens and describe how accommodation and the pupillary reflex focus near and far images.
  17. Phototransduction. Trace phototransduction from photon absorption by retinal through the change in cyclic GMP to the decrease in glutamate release and explain why photoreceptors hyperpolarize to light.
  18. Rods cones and visual processing. Compare rods and cones by sensitivity and acuity and trace the visual pathway to predict the field deficit produced by a lesion at the optic nerve or the optic chiasm.
  19. Refractive errors and clinical vision. Explain myopia, hyperopia, presbyopia, and astigmatism in terms of focal point and eyeball or lens shape and state the corrective lens for each.
  20. Auditory transduction. Trace sound from the tympanic membrane through the ossicles and cochlear fluid to hair cell bending and auditory nerve firing and explain how pitch and loudness are coded.
  21. Conductive and sensorineural hearing loss. Distinguish conductive from sensorineural hearing loss by the site of the lesion and by the expected Weber and Rinne results.
  22. Equilibrium. Explain how the semicircular canals detect angular acceleration and how the utricle and saccule detect linear acceleration and head position.
  23. Taste and smell. Compare gustatory and olfactory transduction and explain why olfactory input reaches the limbic system without a thalamic relay.
  24. Vision testing. Perform visual acuity, blind spot, accommodation, and color vision testing and interpret each result.
  25. Hearing and equilibrium testing. Perform Weber and Rinne tuning fork tests and a balance test and interpret the findings.
