Week 4 · Draw along sheet

Integration at the synapse: draw along

One blank drawing for each part of the story. Draw the water levels, the arrows, the plus and minus signs and the labels as we go, then write what happens and why. Use one colour for everything that raises the level (the cups, excitatory inputs, plus signs) and a second colour for everything that lowers it or holds it down (the leak, the scoops, inhibitory inputs).

Part 1. The bucket

You tip in one cup. Does the water reach the line? What happens to that water if you wait?

Draw the arrows and labels as we go. Then write what happens and why.

Part 2. Only small cups

Using small cups only, how could you get the water up to the line? Find two different ways.

Draw the arrows and labels as we go. Then write what happens and why.

Part 3. Turn the bucket into a neuron

In the neuron, what is the cup? What is the leak? What is the line?

Draw the arrows and labels as we go. Then write what happens and why.

Part 4. One input is not enough

What are the two ways this neuron could reach threshold using only small EPSPs?

Draw the arrows and labels as we go. Then write what happens and why.

Part 5. Someone scoops water out

When the new potassium key hole door opens, which way does potassium go, and what does that do to the inside?

Draw the arrows and labels as we go. Then write what happens and why.

Part 6. A second kind of scoop: chloride

A cup arrives and the inside starts to become more positive. Which way does chloride move, and what does that do to the cup?

Draw the arrows and labels as we go. Then write what happens and why.

Part 7. The cell adds everything

What does the trigger zone compare with the line: each input one at a time, or something else?

Draw the arrows and labels as we go. Then write what happens and why.

Part 8. Where the cup comes from matters

A and C release the same amount. Which counts for more at the trigger zone? Where would a scoop have the most effect?

Draw the arrows and labels as we go. Then write what happens and why.

Part 9. Turn down one hose without touching the bucket

What does less calcium do to the message from A? Does the receiving cell respond differently to B and C?

Draw the arrows and labels as we go. Then write what happens and why.

Part 10. An input that is used a lot gets a bigger cup

The same message has to give a bigger change. What could the receiving cell put more of at this synapse?

Draw the arrows and labels as we go. Then write what happens and why.

Part 11. A different kind of connection: a tunnel

Positive charge has just come into cell 1. What happens in cell 2, and how fast? What do you gain with a tunnel, and what do you give up?

Draw the arrows and labels as we go. Then write what happens and why.

Part 12. Take away all the scoops

All inhibition is gone and nothing was added to the excitatory side. What happens when the ordinary inputs arrive?

Draw the arrows and labels as we go. Then write what happens and why.

Tell the whole story from memory

Close everything else. Fill in each box in order, in your own words.
