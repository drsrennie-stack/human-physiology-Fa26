What you are drawing

Holding blood pressure steady when you stand up

When you stand, gravity pulls about half a liter of blood into the veins of your legs and belly. Draw what normally keeps your blood pressure steady, what goes wrong when that response is too slow or too weak, and what fixes it.

Your three panels

  1. Normal. The baroreceptor reflex working on time when a healthy person stands up: the sensors, the nerves that carry the signal in, the control center, the nerves that carry the signal out, and each effector.
  2. Disrupted. Standing up quickly when the reflex is slow or weak, so the pressure stays low and the person feels lightheaded (orthostatic hypotension).
  3. The fix. The reflex response that brings the pressure back, plus at least one thing the person can do to help it.
