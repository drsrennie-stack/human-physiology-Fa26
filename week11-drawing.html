What you are drawing

Out of breath on the stairs

Your kidneys check how much oxygen your blood is delivering and adjust how many red blood cells your bone marrow makes. Draw that loop working, what happens when the kidneys are damaged and cannot make enough of the signal, and what fixes it.

Your three panels

  1. Normal. The erythropoietin (EPO) loop in a healthy person whose oxygen delivery drops: the sensor, the signal, where it goes, what it makes, and how the loop turns itself off.
  2. Disrupted. Chronic kidney disease: the damaged kidneys make too little EPO, so the person stays anemic, tired and short of breath on the stairs even though their tissues are short of oxygen.
  3. The fix. EPO given as a medicine (a drug made to act like the body's own EPO), with iron, and what you predict will happen to red cell production over the following weeks.
