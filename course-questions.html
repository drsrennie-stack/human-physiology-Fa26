Getting started

Where do I start?

Open the week's Start here page in Canvas, or the week page on the course site, and follow the steps in order. From Week 4 on, every week runs the same loop (Weeks 1 to 3 keep the steps on their own pages):

  1. The pre-read: a quick look at the headings and figures, with a few short questions. Turned in.
  2. First pass, in your first color: work through the week's lessons and videos in order, filling each box of your Competency Study Guide as you go.
  3. Second pass, in your second color, on the same guide you started, printed or on your own paper: go back through the same lessons and add what you missed and fix what was wrong. Do not erase the first color.
  4. Upload your Competency Study Guide with both colors on it.
  5. Study it for several days: Rx Cards, brain dumps, drawing from memory, Study With Me.
  6. The Mastery Check, and upload the report.
  7. The lab: PhysioEx, then the worksheet.
  8. Your application case, worked into your patient chart.
  9. Your patient chart: this week's numbers and your thinking.
  10. The discussion: post by Friday, replies by Sunday.

Everything is due Sunday at 10:00 pm, except the first discussion post, which is due Friday.

Grading

How is my grade calculated?

Four categories. Show Me What You Know, the two exams, 35 percent. Investigate It, the labs, 25 percent. Use It, your application cases and patient chart, 25 percent. Think About It, the discussions, 15 percent. The pre-read, the Competency Study Guide and the Mastery Check carry no points on purpose; you still turn them in each week and they are marked complete or not complete. Full detail in the syllabus.

What is the patient chart?

Two patients you follow by hand, one at a time. Camila Reyes carries Weeks 1 to 8 and Dale Whitcomb carries Weeks 9 to 15. Each week you copy that week’s numbers into the patient’s flowsheets and add what changed, your problem list, a drawing and your thinking, including what you worked out in that week’s application case. Nothing is uploaded weekly.

Each patient’s analysis is a recorded chart walk: you on camera with only your handwritten chart, reasoning out loud from your own numbers for 10 to 15 minutes, recorded in Canvas Studio and turned in with its transcript. Camila’s chart and chart walk are turned in on Sunday, November 1, with Midterm 1, and Midterm 1 includes a question about her, answered from memory. Dale’s chart and chart walk are due Wednesday, December 16. Each is half of the 5 percent. See what you turn in, and when.

What is the Mastery Check, and when does it count?

A practice exam you build yourself in the Mastery Check. An attempt counts when it covers one week, has at least 50 questions, tests every competency that week, and you score 80 percent or higher. The report says Met or Not yet for each of those. Take it as many times as you like; each try is a fresh set of questions. When an attempt meets the standard, save the report as a PDF and upload it. It carries no points, and it is marked complete or not complete.

Is there a curve?

No. The scale is fixed: A is 89.5 to 100, B is 79.5 to 89.4, C is 69.5 to 79.4, D is 59.5 to 69.4.

Is there extra credit?

Not in the back-end sense. There is nothing you can do in December to pull a grade up. What this course has instead is Scholar Points: up to a 2.5 percent bump on your final grade, earned across the term by putting hours into practicing with other people. Thirty hours earns the full 2.5, fewer earns that proportion, and three hours a week is the most that counts.

What grade do I need for nursing programs?

Most health programs require a B or better in this course, and many effectively require an A. Check your specific program's requirements with a counselor: Yuba counseling.

Can I take this Pass or No Pass?

No. This course is letter grade only.

How are Competency Study Guides graded?

They are not graded for content. You upload the guide each week with both colors on it, and it is marked complete or not complete. What matters is that both passes are there and honest, because the gap between your two colors is what tells you what to study.

How are discussions graded?

One post a week, due Friday at 10:00 pm, and replies due Sunday at 10:00 pm. Most weeks the post asks what your Mastery Check showed you, what you did about it, and what happened when you tried again, plus one question about the physiology. Weeks 4 and 5 are an exam practice instead: see the question about the exam practice. The Canvas discussion states the details each week.

How is the lab graded?

Both parts, always: PhysioEx must show complete in Pearson, and you turn in the worksheet and data record, plus the clinical correlation questions in weeks that have them. One without the other earns nothing.

Are the book problems graded?

No. Book problems carry no points and nothing is submitted. They are listed on the Week 1 to 3 pages as practice, and there are two ways to use them: work them forwards if you have a way in, or work them backwards by reading a solution and writing why each step is there, then reproducing it from blank paper.

How much is each midterm worth?

The two exams together are 35 percent, so each one is 17.5 percent of your final grade.

What is the biggest part of my grade?

The exams, at 35 percent. But the weekly work is 65 percent combined, and it is also the practice for the exams, so the split is less lopsided than it looks. Labs and applications together are half your grade.

Can my grade be rounded up?

The scale already has the rounding built in: 89.5 is an A. Beyond that, no, because an exception for one student is an unfairness to everybody else.

Do you drop the lowest anything?

No drops. Every week counts, which is also why no single week can sink you.

What happens if I bomb one midterm?

One midterm is 17.5 percent of the grade, and there is a lot of the grade left. Find the gap with the Mastery Check, come to office hours, and fix it before the next block, because physiology compounds.

Where can I see exactly what is graded?

The component table in syllabus section 07, and the Competency Packet for exactly what content is fair game. Nothing outside the packet is graded.

Why do the Competency Study Guides carry no points?

Because they are where you find out what you do not know, and that only works if the guide is honest rather than tidy. They are what makes everything else possible, and they double as your midterm study guide, so the payoff comes on the exam, not on the sheet. You still upload it each week, marked complete or not complete.

Do typed assignments count?

No. Unless an assignment says otherwise, everything you turn in is handwritten and hand drawn. Typed work earns a zero. See syllabus section 11.

Why handwritten?

Drawing a pathway by hand is how your brain builds it, and it is how the midterms work, so it is how the practice works.

How fast are things graded?

Weekly work is typically graded within the week after it comes in. Midterm videos take longer; watch Canvas announcements.

Can I resubmit something to raise the grade?

No. Graded work stays graded. The next week is always the better investment.

What does mastery based mean here?

Attending and turning things in does not by itself earn the grade. The study hours and what you can actually do are what do.

Where does participation show up in the grade?

Participation in an online course is submitted work. Each week that means the pre-read, your Competency Study Guide, your Mastery Check report, your lab, your application case and your discussion. Census is September 27, and I certify who is actively participating from what has been turned in.

Are the weights ever adjusted during the term?

No. The four categories and their weights are fixed for the term: 35, 25, 25 and 15. What you see in the syllabus is the whole deal.

What are Scholar Points?

Up to a 2.5 percent bump on your final course grade, earned by hours spent practicing this material with other people: Study With Me sessions, study groups you set up, quizzing and teaching each other. Thirty hours earns the full 2.5 percent and fewer earns that proportion. Three hours a week is the most that counts, so it cannot be done in one weekend. Details on the Scholar Points page.

Competency Study Guides

What is a Competency Study Guide?

A weekly guide with one box for each competency you must be able to do that week, and two prompts for each box to choose from. From Week 4 on, you choose how to do it: print the ready-made worksheet, or work the same prompts from the competency list on your own paper. You fill it in two passes on those same pages, one color each, as you work through the week's lessons, then upload it. It is your study guide for the rest of the week and for the midterm.

What is the pre-read?

The first step of every week, about 30 minutes. A quick look ahead, not a full read: you look at the headings and figures in that week's chapters and answer a few short questions, so the lessons make more sense when you get to them. Fill it in on screen or on the printable copy, then upload it. It is marked complete or not complete.

When is the Competency Study Guide due?

Sunday at 10:00 pm, with everything else.

Is my Competency Study Guide graded for accuracy?

No. It is marked complete or not complete. What counts is that both passes are there, in two colors, from your own work.

Can I type my Competency Study Guide?

No. Handwritten and hand drawn, then photographed or scanned into a PDF for Canvas.

What does a good Competency Study Guide answer look like?

Two colors telling the truth. First color: what you built on your first pass through the lessons. Second color: what you added or fixed on your second pass. A drawing or a sequence you could teach from, not a copied sentence. If your guide can carry you through a brain dump without the book, it is good.

Where do I find each week's Competency Study Guide?

On the week's page and in the week's Canvas module, as a printable PDF and on screen, posted when the week opens. The printable one says Competency Study Guide at the top.

Do Competency Study Guides help with the midterms?

They are the midterm study guide. Every midterm pathway starts life as a Competency Study Guide prompt.

What if I cannot finish every prompt?

Upload what you have by Sunday at 10:00 pm. Partial beats nothing, and the late policy applies after the deadline.

Can I work on Competency Study Guides with classmates?

Study together, absolutely. But what you turn in must be your own work in your own hand from your own head.

How long should a Competency Study Guide take?

Plan on three to four hours for the first pass and 30 to 60 minutes for the second, spread across a few sittings early in the week.

What is the two color rule?

Your first color is your first pass through the week's lessons and videos. Your second color is everything you add or fix on the second pass, on the same pages, whether you started on the printed worksheet or on your own paper. Never erase the first color. The result reads at a glance: the second color is exactly what you still need to commit to memory, and that is where your studying goes.

Do I need special supplies?

Paper, pens in two colors, and a way to photograph or scan to PDF. That is it.

My handwriting is bad. Will I lose points?

No. Legible is the bar, beautiful is not. If I can read it and follow the drawing, it grades fine.

Is there a Competency Study Guide in a midterm week?

What if I lose my Competency Study Guide before submitting?

This is why you photograph work the moment you finish it. Rebuild what you can before 10:00 pm and submit; partial beats zero, and the late policy has no lost paper clause.

Discussions

How do discussions work?

One post a week, not two. It works through something from that week's physiology and says something honest about your own thinking on that same material, one post that carries both.

When are discussion posts due?

Initial post Friday at 10:00 pm, so there is something to reply to. Peer replies Sunday at 10:00 pm.

What does the honest thinking part look like?

Where did your prediction turn out to be wrong, and what were you assuming that made it seem right? What did you have to go back and relearn? Which part still feels shaky when you explain it without looking?

How many replies do I owe?

Peer replies are due Sunday at 10:00 pm; the assignment in Canvas states the count. Replies that engage the physiology count; replies that say great post do not.

Can I post my discussion late?

The late policy applies: up to 24 hours late is an automatic 50 percent, after that a zero. And a late initial post also shortchanges your classmates who needed something to reply to.

What should I write about?

Anything real from that week: a loop that surprised you, a prediction that failed, a mechanism that finally clicked. Specific beats general every time.

Is there a length requirement?

The Canvas assignment states any minimum. The real bar is substance: physiology plus your thinking, in your own words.

Can I use AI to write my discussion post?

No. Discussions are graded work, and graded work is your own words from your own head. Use AI to study; write the post yourself.

Why is Friday the deadline for the first post?

So the weekend conversation exists. If everyone posts Sunday at 9:58 pm, nobody replies to anyone.

Do discussions cover exam material?

They are built from the same week's competencies, so yes, working them is studying.

Where are the discussions?

In Canvas, in that week's module. The week page's Discuss step reminds you every week.

Do replies have deadlines different from the post?

Yes: initial post Friday 10:00 pm, peer replies Sunday 10:00 pm, every week, no variation.

Can I see classmates' posts before I write mine?

Midterms

How do the midterms work?

Each midterm has three multiple choice questions, and you record yourself for the whole of each one. You get 10 minutes to plan and build a physiological model on a whiteboard, from memory with no notes, then 5 minutes to teach it out loud, and you finish by giving your answer. The model and your teaching are worth 75 percent of the points for that question, and your answer is worth 25 percent. The model shows the mechanism: label it, explain each step and say why it happens.

When is Midterm 1?

The window runs Thursday, October 29 at 8:00 am to Sunday, November 1 at 10:00 pm. It covers Weeks 1 to 7. Week 8 has no new teaching, so Monday to Wednesday is yours to review before the window opens.

When is Midterm 2?

Monday, December 14 at 8:00 am to Wednesday, December 16 at 10:00 pm, at the front of Week 15. It covers Weeks 9 to 14.

Is the midterm at a set time?

No. Each midterm is a window, not an hour. Midterm 1 opens Thursday, October 29 at 8:00 am and closes Sunday, November 1 at 10:00 pm. Midterm 2 opens Monday, December 14 at 8:00 am and closes Wednesday, December 16 at 10:00 pm. You pick when inside the window.

Can I use notes on the midterm?

No notes, no book, nothing open, including during the 10 minutes you spend building your model. You, a whiteboard and your own head, on camera the whole time. That is the point of the format.

How do I record my video?

Record in Canvas Studio, with your phone or laptop camera set where it sees you and the whole whiteboard. Keep recording for the whole question: the 10 minutes of building your model and the 5 minutes of teaching it.

Then turn in the transcript with each video. In Canvas Studio, open the video, go to the Captions tab and choose Request, with English as the language. When the captions are ready, use Review and Publish, fix only the words the machine got wrong, such as drug names and physiology terms, and Download the caption file. Do not reword what you said; the transcript has to match the video.

How long should my video be?

About 15 minutes for each question: 10 minutes to build your model and 5 minutes to teach it, ending with your answer. There are three questions on each midterm.

What if I mess up mid recording?

Pause, correct yourself, and keep teaching, exactly like a real teacher would. A corrected mistake shows understanding. Re record if you must, inside the window.

What gets asked on a midterm?

Three multiple choice questions built from that block's competencies in the packet. If you can build and teach the model behind each competency from memory, you are ready.

How do I know the format before it counts?

Weeks 4 and 5 are a full practice run of the format before it counts. And every week's study step practices the model part: building a mechanism from memory and explaining it out loud.

Are the midterms cumulative?

Each covers its own block of weeks. Midterm 1 is Weeks 1 to 7, Midterm 2 is Weeks 9 to 14. But physiology compounds, so Midterm 2's pathways stand on everything before them.

What if my internet dies during the window?

Each window is several days long so a bad day cannot sink you. Record early and submit early.

Can I retake a midterm?

No retakes and no reopening the window for one student that everyone else met. If something serious is happening in your life, talk to me about your options in the course as a whole.

How should I study for a midterm?

Draw pathways from memory, then teach them out loud with no notes, then check the drawing against your Competency Study Guide. The Mastery Check report tells you which pathways to hit first.

Do the midterms test lab skills?

Yes, lab reasoning shows up in the teach backs: reading a trace, predicting what a change does, explaining a value.

Is there a final exam?

No. There are two exams and Midterm 2 is your last assessment. Its window is Monday December 14 to Wednesday December 16, and the term simply ends there.

What is a draw and teach exam like the first time?

Strange for about three minutes, then normal. That is why the weekly practice rounds exist. Students consistently report the format shows what they know better than any bubble sheet did.

Can someone else be in the room when I record?

You must be alone with no help of any kind during the exam. The video is you teaching, solo.

What if I freeze on camera?

Breathe, look at your drawing, and start with what the pathway accomplishes. The practice rounds exist so that by exam week your hands know the drill even when your nerves do not.

When do midterm grades come back?

Videos take longer to grade than sheets. Watch Canvas announcements for the timeline after each window closes.

What happens to regular work during the midterm weeks?

Week 8 has no new teaching: Monday to Wednesday is yours to review, and Midterm 1 runs Thursday to Sunday. Midterm 2 runs during the three days of Week 15.

Can I choose which question I get?

No. The exam gives you the questions. Your model is how you work out each answer, and every question comes from the competencies you have already studied.

What supplies do I need for the exam video?

A whiteboard and markers, decent light, and a phone propped where it sees you and the whole board. Set them up before you press record.

Studying

What is the best way to study for this course?

Retrieve, do not reread. Draw pathways from memory, teach them out loud, run your recall cards, and keep an error log of every wrong answer. Rereading feels productive and is not; the research on this is decades deep and very consistent.

How do I use Rx Cards?

Open Rx Cards every day and clear what is due. Reviews are never capped, only new cards are, so the pile stays a size you can carry. Little and often beats big and rare, because a card cannot move forward twice in one day.

What is the Brain Dump tool?

The Brain Dump picks a random competency and prompt for you. Pick a week and press Spin. Do the prompt on paper, from memory, with everything closed. Then press Check my work and tick only what is actually on your paper against the list of what the prompt asked for. The competencies you keep leaving pieces out of collect in a weakest list, so you know what to drill next.

What is Draw It to Know It?

A drawing exercise in the Mastery OS. It asks how sure you feel about a competency, then gives you a prompt: draw the mechanism or build a small map from memory on the canvas, then open the self check list and mark what you covered. When you went in confident and came out patchy, it moves that competency up your weak spot list.

Where are all the study tools in one place?

Press Course tools in the bottom left corner of any course page. Your study tools are at the top: Rx Cards, the Brain Dump, Study With Me, the Mastery Check and Draw It to Know It.

What are recall cards?

Short retrieval questions built from the competencies. Answer before you flip. Getting it wrong then correcting is the studying; smooth rereading is not.

What is the gap finder?

The OS view that shows which competencies are weak right now, so your next hour goes where it earns the most.

How often should I run cards?

Daily beats weekly at the same total minutes. Ten focused minutes a day keeps three hundred cards warm.

Should I make my own flashcards too?

No, and this is deliberate: do not make flashcards from your notes, organize the notes instead, so you always know where to find things. The OS recall cards already cover the 268 competencies; your scarce hours are better spent answering prompts, drawing, and teaching.

How do I memorize pathways?

You do not memorize them, you rebuild them. Draw from a blank page, check against your Competency Study Guide, fix, and draw again tomorrow. Two or three rebuilds and it is yours.

What does teach it out loud actually do?

Speaking forces sequence and causality. The gaps you can hide from your eyes you cannot hide from your own ears. It is also literally the exam format.

Is rereading the textbook studying?

It is warming up. Rereading and rewatching are passive, and passive time feels like progress while producing very little of it. The Silverthorn reading feeds the Competency Study Guide; retrieval, drawing from memory, and explaining out loud are the studying.

How do I study with the videos?

Watch each concept's video during your first pass, alongside its lesson, and again whenever a part is still unclear. In the walkthroughs, look for the Stuck? Watch a short explanation button. Fill that competency's box in your first color as you go, then add and fix in your second color on your second pass.

What should Sunday review look like?

Clear your Rx Cards, retry this week's error log rows cold, do a brain dump or two, and skim next week's Start here page so Monday starts warm.

Can I study with other people?

Yes, and teaching each other pathways is the best group activity in this course. Just remember the turned in work is yours alone.

What do I do with a concept that will not stick?

Draw it, teach it to the wall, ask Hootie or the discussion board, bring it to Wednesday office hours, and put its cards on daily rotation. Stubborn concepts fall to repetition plus a second explanation.

Is AI allowed for studying?

Encouraged. Have it quiz you, explain a concept a second way, or listen to you teach and ask follow ups. For graded work, your own words in your own hand, always.

How is studying physiology different from anatomy?

Anatomy asks what and where; physiology asks how and what happens if. Study by predicting and explaining, not listing. If you can answer what happens if this goes up, you are studying correctly.

What is the exam practice in Weeks 4 and 5?

A full practice run of the midterm format before it counts, on material from Week 2.

Week 4. Two multiple choice questions, one at a time, one video each: 15 minutes preparing your answer on camera by building a model from memory, then 5 minutes presenting it and giving your answer. The videos go to me only, through an assignment. Then you post in Discussion 4 about how it went, where you got stuck, and what you will do about it, and you help each other with solutions.

Week 5. The rubrics and the answer key come out. You score your own models, answers and presenting, and analyze what it shows you in Discussion 5.

How do I know if I am actually ready for a midterm?

Draw and teach each pathway in the block from a blank page with no notes. Where you can, you are ready. Where you cannot, that is the list.

What is an error log and should I keep one?

Yes, keep one. Every wrong answer is a question you do not yet know how to ask. Log one row per miss: what I thought, what is correct, why I was wrong, what I will remember. A week later, try the same question again without looking. Retire a row only when you can get its whole type right cold, not the first time you get it right by luck.

How do I learn from a graded exam?

Sit with the graded exam and classify every lost point by error type: did not study it, studied but did not understand it, understood but could not apply it, was missing a foundation from an earlier course, or misread the question. Each type has a different fix, and until you know which type you are losing points to, you are guessing at what to change. Fifteen honest minutes per exam.

Does spacing really beat cramming?

At the same total hours, yes, and it is not close. One day of cramming and seven days of spaced work can contain identical hours and score very differently, because memory is built by returning to material after partly forgetting it. This is why the recall cards run daily and why the weekly rhythm exists.

How do I know if my studying is active or passive?

Rate an hour honestly on a five point scale: rereading and watching are passive, retrieval, self testing, and explaining out loud are active. If most of your hours sit at the passive end, that, more than total hours, is usually the problem. Move one hour a day from rereading to drawing from memory and watch what happens.

Why does effective studying feel strange at first?

Because it conflicts with how you have always studied. Testing yourself before you feel ready, drawing from a blank page, logging your errors, all of it feels harder and less smooth than rereading. The strangeness is not a sign the method is wrong. It is a sign that what you have always done is not what the research recommends. Give it two honest weeks.

Succeeding online, anywhere

What makes online classes harder than in person?

Nothing about the content. What is missing is the schedule someone else built: the room, the seat time, the classmates who notice your absence. Online students who succeed rebuild those on purpose.

What is the single most important online habit?

A written weekly schedule with your study blocks placed like shifts, made Sunday for the week ahead, and protected like a job.

How do I make myself start when nobody is watching?

Shrink the start: open the page, set a timer for ten minutes, begin the first prompt. Starting is the hard part; momentum does the rest. Never wait to feel ready.

Where should I study?

Same place, same times, phone out of reach. Your brain learns that the chair means work. A kitchen table at a fixed hour beats a perfect desk used at random.

How do I stop procrastinating in an online class?

Deadlines you set beat deadlines you dread: give every task your own due date two days before the real one. And do the dreaded task first each session while your will is fresh.

How often should I check in on an online course?

Log in at least three times a week even when nothing is due. Online courses punish the out of sight, out of mind reflex more than anything else.

How do I take an online class seriously while working?

Tell the people in your life your study blocks like you would tell them shifts. The class is real; treat its hours as real and others will too.

What do successful online students do that others skip?

They read the syllabus once completely, they submit early, they email professors like professionals, and they ask questions the same week the confusion starts.

How do I email a professor properly?

Course and section in the subject, your full name, one clear question, and what you already tried. Professors move fastest for students who make the question easy to answer.

What is netiquette and does it matter?

Write online the way you would speak in a seminar: respectful, on topic, no all caps, disagree with ideas rather than people. It matters because your classmates are real and your words are permanent.

How do I stay motivated for fifteen weeks?

Attach the course to the reason you enrolled, and keep the reason visible: the program you are entering, the patient you will someday treat. Then trust the routine on the days motivation does not show up, because routine outlasts mood.

How do I avoid falling behind without noticing?

A written self check, two minutes every Sunday: did everything go in, what is my current standing, what is next week, and what will I change. Students chronically overestimate their own understanding, and the gap is largest exactly where the stakes are highest. Casual reflection, I think I know this, does not close that gap. Written and specific does.

What do I do the day life blows up my plan?

Salvage the highest value hour: submit whatever is closest to done, message the professor factually and briefly, and rebuild next week's plan the same day. One bad day becomes a bad month only through silence.

Are online classes easier?

No, and believing they are is the most expensive myth in higher education. Same units, same hours, minus the built in structure. Plan accordingly and they are absolutely passable.

How do I build classmates online?

Use the discussions like a room: reply to the same few people, answer questions you know, form a two person message thread with someone whose posts you respect. Two allies change a term.
