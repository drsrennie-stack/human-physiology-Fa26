What this builds on

Work through these before you claim a case. Every level asks you to write where your work comes from: a video, a notes section, a textbook part, or a reputable source you looked up.

Reputable sources to look things up: PubMed (opens in a new tab) (research articles; write the title, journal and year), UpToDate (a clinical reference doctors use; check whether the college library gives you access), and MedlinePlus Medical Tests (opens in a new tab) (free, from the National Library of Medicine). A random website, a social media post or an AI answer does not count as a source by itself.

How it works

One patient, one heartbeat

  1. Claim a case below. Your patient’s numbers appear.
  2. Print the Wiggers diagram template. It shows a normal heartbeat in light gray: aortic, left ventricular and left atrial pressure, ventricular volume, the EKG and the heart sounds. Draw your patient’s curves over it in pen.
  3. Mark up the big diagram for your patient. Then, beside it, draw a pair of small graphs for each phase your case names (these are the side-by-side sketches in your case): the normal heartbeat on the left and your patient on the right, at the same scale. Each small graph is a close-up of just that phase, with the left ventricle, aorta and left atrium pressure curves and the volume curve. Mark where each valve opens or closes.
  4. Explain each change in writing. Take a photo of the finished drawing, then get your twist and redraw what it changes.
