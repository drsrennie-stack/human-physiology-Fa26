Station 1

Take a blood pressure by sound

Watch, from Stanford Medicine 25: How to Measure Pulsus Paradoxus (Real Patient!) (opens in a new tab) (2:59). It uses the same cuff and the same Korotkoff sounds you use here. Watch for how the systolic pressure changes when the patient breathes in.

Use headphones. If you cannot use sound, check Flash with each sound.

  1. Press Inflate. The cuff pumps up above the systolic pressure and then lets air out slowly, about 3 mmHg per heartbeat.
  2. Watch the needle and listen.
  3. Press First sound when the tapping starts.
  4. Press Sounds gone when the tapping stops.

You pass a patient when both of your readings are within 5 mmHg of the true values.

Station 2

The ankle-brachial index

Watch, from Stanford Medicine 25: Ankle Brachial Index (ABI) Test: How to Perform (opens in a new tab) (7:44). Watch for where the ankle pressures are taken and which arm pressure goes into the calculation.

When a leg artery is narrowed, the blood pressure past the narrow spot drops. Comparing the systolic pressure at the ankle with the systolic pressure in the arm shows how well blood reaches the legs.

  1. For each leg, take the higher of its two ankle pressures (dorsalis pedis or posterior tibial, two arteries at the ankle).
  2. Divide it by the higher of the two arm pressures.
  3. Round to two decimal places and type it in the box. Then choose what it means from the list.
What the index means (resting, adults)
IndexMeaning
above 1.40Stiff arteries that the cuff cannot squeeze shut (often from diabetes or kidney disease); the reading is not reliable
1.00 to 1.40Normal
0.91 to 0.99Borderline
0.90 or belowPeripheral artery disease
0.40 or belowSevere peripheral artery disease
