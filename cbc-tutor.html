What it is

A CBC counts the cells in a sample of blood.

CBC stands for complete blood count. It is one of the most common laboratory tests.

What it counts

It reports on three kinds of cells.

  • Red blood cells (erythrocytes) carry oxygen.
  • White blood cells (leukocytes) defend against infection.
  • Platelets (thrombocytes) start blood clots.

White blood cells

White blood cells come in five types.

  • Neutrophil
  • Lymphocyte
  • Monocyte
  • Eosinophil
  • Basophil

The differential reports the percentage of each type.

Extra clues

  • A high RDW means the red cells vary in size ().
  • A low MCHC means the red cells are pale ().
  • Hematocrit is about 3 times hemoglobin when cells are normal.

The math

  • MCV = hematocrit ÷ RBC × 10
  • MCH = hemoglobin ÷ RBC × 10
  • MCHC = hemoglobin ÷ hematocrit × 100
  • ANC = WBC × (neutrophils % + bands %) ÷ 100

Calculator

Set up the formula, calculate, then interpret

The laboratory measures hemoglobin, hematocrit and the cell counts directly. The red cell indices and the absolute neutrophil count are calculated from them.

Worked example

  • A patient has a hematocrit of 29.0% and an RBC count of 3.90 ×10⁶/µL.
  • Set up the formula: MCV = hematocrit ÷ RBC count × 10.
  • Calculate: 29.0 ÷ 3.90 × 10 = 74.4 fL.
  • Interpret: 74.4 fL is below 80 fL, so the red blood cells are microcytic (smaller than normal).

Left shift and right shift

Both describe neutrophils. A young neutrophil has a nucleus with one curved piece. As it ages, the nucleus pinches into more lobes. Sort neutrophils by lobe count, youngest on the left, and you get the graph below.

Different topic, same words: the oxygen-hemoglobin curve also shifts left or right. That is about oxygen binding, not cell counts.

Percent is not the same as count

  • The differential shows the proportion of each cell type, not the actual number.
  • When one cell type falls, the percentages of the others rise even if their numbers have not changed.
  • Multiply the percentage by the total WBC count to find the absolute count.
