Why this is not extra credit

Up to a 2.5 percent bump on your final course grade, earned by putting real hours into practicing this material with other people. Thirty hours earns the full bump. Three hours a week is the most that can count.

I do not offer extra credit. Extra credit is a back end fix: something bolted on at the end of the term so a grade that went badly can be pulled up, usually by work that has little to do with the course. It rewards whoever is most behind and it teaches nobody anything.

Scholar Points work from the front end. They are not a rescue and they are not a second chance. They are what happens when you put the hours into the practice that makes this course easier while you are still taking it: saying the physiology out loud, teaching it to somebody, being quizzed by somebody who will not let you off. Students who do that tend to have a better grade before any bump is applied, and the bump is on top of that.

Which is the honest sales pitch for it. The 2.5 percent is real and I will apply it, but it is the smaller half of what you get. The larger half is that the exams get easier because you spent the term producing this material out loud instead of rereading it.

How it works

  • Thirty hours over the semester earns the full 2.5 percent. Fewer hours earn a proportion of it. Nothing is all or nothing.
  • Three hours a week is the most that counts. You can do more and you are welcome to, but a single marathon weekend cannot substitute for showing up across the term. Spacing is the whole reason this works.
  • Hosts get one extra hour. Running a session means preparing it and facilitating it, and that work is real. A two hour session you hosted logs as three. Not double, just the one extra hour on top, however long the session ran. Attendees log the time they were there.
  • The three hour weekly cap is applied after the host hour. So a two hour session you hosted fills the week on its own. Anything past that still goes on your log, it just does not add to the total.
  • It is applied at the end of the semester, to your final course percentage, after everything else is in.
  • It can only help you. There is no version of this that costs you anything, and choosing not to do it is a perfectly reasonable choice that leaves your grade exactly where your work put it.

What counts as an hour

Time spent working the physiology online with at least one other person: a Study With Me session, a study group you set up yourself, quizzing each other, teaching a competency at a whiteboard on camera, doing brain dumps together, running a Kahoot, working the book problems together. It does not include time you spent alone, and it does not include doing graded work together, which is not allowed anyway.

What a session has to do to count

None of this is here to be difficult. It is here because I am putting a percentage on your grade, and a percentage on a grade has to rest on something I can actually see.

  • Online, not in person. Zoom, Google Meet, Discord, anything you like that records. An in person study session is still worth doing, it just cannot be verified, so it does not go on the log.
  • Record it. Whoever hosts records the session and keeps the link. That is what shows me it happened and that people were working, not just signed in.
  • Cameras on. Everybody, for the whole session. A black square is not attendance, and a session where half the squares are black is not the thing this is trying to build.
  • Stay the whole time. Log the time you were actually present. Arriving late or leaving early is fine, log what you were there for. Do not log an hour you spent in another tab.
  • One session at a time. Sitting in two rooms at once means neither can be verified, so neither counts.
  • Hosts keep the recording and the attendance list. If you ran it, you are the one who can show who was there and for how long.

If something about this is a problem for you, cameras especially, come and talk to me rather than skipping the whole thing. There is almost always a way to sort it out.

Keeping track

Log each session as you do it, right after it ends, while you still remember who was there and what you worked on. Keep the log yourself, on paper or in a document you own. Nothing about it is uploaded anywhere except what you turn in to Canvas.

What every entry needs

  • The date.
  • What it was: Study With Me attended, Study With Me hosted, a study group you set up, or quizzing and teaching with a classmate.
  • Hours, to the quarter hour. Twenty minutes is 0.33 hours.
  • What you worked on, for example the week and the competency.
  • Who was there. First names are fine.
  • The recording link.
  • Your confirmation that cameras were on and you were present for the time you are logging.

At the end of the semester, send me the whole log and I will apply the bump.

Where you are

Your running total is the one you keep on your own log, and it is the number you carry over each time you submit. Add each block's counted hours to the total from the block before, so the number you turn in is always the term total to date.

Your submitted hours and any feedback on them show up in Canvas with the rest of your work. The bump itself is not in the gradebook during the term. I apply it at the end, to your final course percentage.

Questions people ask

Is 2.5 percent enough to change my letter grade?

Sometimes, at a boundary. An 87.5 becomes a 90. It is not enough to rescue a grade that is well down, and it was never designed to, which is the difference between this and extra credit.

What if I can only manage ten hours?

Then you earn a third of it, which is roughly 0.83 percent, and you also spent ten hours practicing physiology out loud. Both of those are worth having. There is no threshold you have to clear before anything counts.

Can I log time I studied alone?

No. Studying alone is most of what this course asks of you and it is already reflected in your grade through the work itself. This is specifically for the hours you spend working it with other people, because that is the part nobody does unless something nudges them.

Why does the host get an extra hour?

Because preparing a session and running it is work that happens outside the session. Building a Kahoot, picking the competency, keeping four people on track for an hour. One extra hour, whatever the length. It is not doubled.

What if somebody cannot have their camera on?

Come and talk to me. There are real reasons, and none of them should cost you the hours. What I cannot do is take a room full of black squares as evidence that a session happened.

Do you check?

I read the logs. I am not auditing timestamps, and an honor system that somebody games mostly cheats them out of the hours, which were the actual point. Put down what you did.

What if I host and two people come for twenty minutes?

Log the time you were there working. Twenty minutes is 0.33 hours, and small sessions are real sessions.
