What this builds on

Work through these before you claim a case. Every level asks you to write where your work comes from: a video, a notes section, a textbook part, or a reputable source you looked up.

Reputable sources to look things up: PubMed (opens in a new tab) (research articles; write the title, journal and year), UpToDate (a clinical reference doctors use; check whether the college library gives you access), and MedlinePlus Medical Tests (opens in a new tab) (free, from the National Library of Medicine). A random website, a social media post or an AI answer does not count as a source by itself.

How it works

One blood test, one patient

  1. Claim a test below. Your patient and their results appear.
  2. Learn what the test measures and why it changes (the list tells you what to look up).
  3. Draw it by hand: for troponin, CK-MB and myoglobin, the level over time with your patient’s draws plotted; for the others, where the marker comes from and why it changes.
  4. Explain it in writing. Take a photo of the finished drawing, then get your twist and add what it changes.
