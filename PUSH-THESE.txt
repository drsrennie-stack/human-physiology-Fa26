THE THREE THINGS YOU ASKED FOR, AND WHERE THEY ARE
----------------------------------------------------------------------

1. CHART IT ON SCREEN, LIKE A HOSPITAL RECORD.
   Banner across the top with record number, allergies underlined, code
   status and isolation. Activity tabs. A time ordered flowsheet with
   grouped rows. A medication record that keeps held drugs on it with
   the word HELD. Orders with their status. It carries no vendor's
   branding, colors or wording anywhere; those are conventions every
   inpatient record shares.

2. WALK THEM THROUGH THE CASE AND THE CHARTING.
   The note arrives one part at a time as steps rather than as a wall,
   and every step that asks for something asks it as a question. Two
   things are held back until the prediction at step 2 is written: the
   Flowsheet and Results tabs, and the rest of the note. At step 2 they
   have the arc and nothing else, so the prediction asks about numbers
   that are genuinely not on the screen. Jumping down the step rail
   opens the note as far as the step jumped to, so the rail is a
   shortcut and never a way round.

3. HOW TO CHART, NOT WHAT TO CHART.
   Twelve charting lessons, one per week: filing against a time,
   charting a weight, a value in a column becoming a row, where a number
   came from, a score charted as its parts, charting what you looked for
   and did not find, an unmeasured loss, a calculated number, closing a
   problem, a held medication, a set value against a measured one, and
   what makes a comparison fair. None of them says what value to type.
   When they file the flowsheet the app checks it against the source and
   says which cells disagree; it never fills one in.

WEEKS 1 AND 2 ARRIVE ALREADY FILED, as you asked. Those two weeks were
charted on paper, so their flowsheets render filled and read only with a
line saying so, and their results panels are in the new file rather than
in the case file, which stays untouched.
