Case 1 · Hemostasis and coagulation

The swollen calf after a long flight

What you know

A 52-year-old flew home from Manila, about 14 hours, mostly sitting. Two days later the left calf is swollen, warm and sore, and it measures 3 cm (1.2 in) wider than the right calf. Breathing is normal and there is no chest pain. Temperature 37.0 °C (98.6 °F). An ultrasound shows a clot in a deep vein behind the knee (a deep vein thrombosis).

Your decision

Compare heparin, which helps antithrombin shut down thrombin and factor Xa; warfarin, which blocks the recycling of vitamin K so the liver makes less active factor II, VII, IX and X; aspirin, which stops platelets from making thromboxane A2; and a clot-dissolving drug (tPA), which turns plasminogen into plasmin. For each one, say what step it acts on, how quickly it works, and its main risk. Choose an approach or a sequence.

Your drawing must show

A vein with the clot in it and the blood barely moving behind a still leg, the platelet plug, the coagulation cascade down to thrombin and fibrin, where calcium and the vitamin K factors act, the body's own brakes (antithrombin and plasmin), and where your intervention acts.

Challenge question

Warfarin takes several days to work fully, so it is usually started together with a faster drug. Why the delay?

Case 2 · Blood types and antibodies

The wrong unit of blood

What you know

A 67-year-old with type A positive blood is getting a transfusion of red cells after surgery. Fifteen minutes in, the patient has chills, a temperature of 38.9 °C (102 °F), pain in the lower back, heart rate 124 beats/min (normal 60 to 100) and blood pressure 86/50 mmHg (normal below 120/80). The urine in the catheter bag turns dark red-brown. The unit turns out to be type B positive, meant for someone else.

Your decision

Compare stopping the transfusion at once and giving IV saline; slowing the transfusion and finishing the unit; giving an antihistamine and finishing the unit; and stopping, then giving type O negative red cells if the patient still needs blood. Choose an approach or a sequence, and say which of these are not physiologically sound and why.

Your drawing must show

The patient's red cells with their A antigens, the donor red cells with their B antigens, the patient's anti-B antibodies, agglutination, complement attacking the donor cells, hemoglobin released into the plasma and reaching the kidney, and where your intervention acts.

Challenge question

This patient has never had a transfusion or been pregnant. Why were anti-B antibodies already in the blood?
