20:10Retrieval practice, and why hard is good

The next level is practicing retrieval. Once you have it down, retrieve it: take the note sheet and draw those prompts again from memory, fixing them so they make more sense to you, practice the brain dumps, do Draw it to know it, or do the recall cards. None of that gives you points, but it is what helps you master the material, and you get to do it in the way you enjoy. I challenge you to try a little of everything. Brain dumps and Draw it to know it are hard, and that is okay; when it is hard, you learn better. The more uncomfortable the effort, the more you learn. I hesitate to say effort, because I see people make a ton of flashcards, and that is a lot of work. You do not have to; I already made about 5,400 for you. But that kind of effort does not pay you in your grade. Think of it like a job: would you rather work hard and get paid, or work hard and not get paid? Some of what gets recommended in education, make all the flashcards, read cover to cover, watch every lecture, I am not saying do not do those things. My opinion is that the timing matters. Doing them on the front end is often less effective than on the back end, which is why I recommend you work the competencies and build your diagrams first, then go watch the videos and read. I would build them from the notes first, but I am not going to tell you what order to do it in. A lot of people find comfort in watching the videos first and then applying it. Do it the way you are comfortable with. The goal is that you master all of the competencies.
