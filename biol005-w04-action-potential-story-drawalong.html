Week 4 · Draw along sheet

Graded potentials and the action potential: draw along

One blank drawing for each part of the story. Draw the arrows, the plus and minus signs and the labels as we go, then write what happens and why. Use one colour for potassium, and a second colour for sodium, for the charges and for the line on the two graphs.

Part 1. Sodium is waiting outside

What would happen to the inside if we suddenly gave sodium a lot of doors?

Draw the arrows and labels as we go. Then write what happens and why.

Part 2. Back to the nucleus: a door with a key

A key binds and the door opens. Which way does sodium go, and what does that do to the inside right there?

Draw the arrows and labels as we go. Then write what happens and why.

Part 3. More keys arrive

If more keys arrive, is the change the same size, smaller or bigger? Why?

Draw the arrows and labels as we go. Then write what happens and why.

Part 4. The change spreads, and it fades

As the change spreads along the inside toward the trigger zone, does it stay the same size?

Draw the arrows and labels as we go. Then write what happens and why.

Part 5. Back to the nucleus: a door that senses voltage

Sodium comes in through the first door. What does that do to the door next to it?

Draw the arrows and labels as we go. Then write what happens and why.

Part 6. What decides whether the loop starts?

The change has faded by the time it reaches the trigger zone. What decides whether the loop starts?

Draw the arrows and labels as we go. Then write what happens and why.

Part 7. What stops a runaway loop?

The door is closed in drawing 1 and closed in drawing 3. Which of the two can be opened by a new signal?

Draw the arrows and labels as we go. Then write what happens and why.

Part 8. Back to the nucleus: a slow potassium door

When this door opens, which way does potassium go, and what does that do to the inside? Why does it matter that this door is slow?

Draw the arrows and labels as we go. Then write what happens and why.

Part 9. Put it in order: one trace

A few keys arrive, and later many keys arrive. Draw what the inside does each time, and label which door is responsible for each part of the line.

Draw the arrows and labels as we go. Then write what happens and why.

Part 10. All or none

Draw the response to each of the three signals. Why does a stronger signal not make a bigger action potential?

Draw the arrows and labels as we go. Then write what happens and why.

Part 11. Did the cell just lose its differences?

After one action potential, does the cell still have lots of sodium outside and lots of potassium inside?

Draw the arrows and labels as we go. Then write what happens and why.
