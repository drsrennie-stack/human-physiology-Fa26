How to use this

This is the paper half of the Week 4 walkthroughs. Print it, then work through it by hand. Drawing something from memory is a different kind of work than clicking through it, and it is the part that shows you what you actually know.

  1. Do each page after you finish the matching walkthrough, not during it.
  2. Draw first, from memory, with the walkthrough closed. Use pencil.
  3. Then reopen the walkthrough and correct your drawing in a second color. What you had to fix is what to study.
  4. Bring the finished pages to class, or photograph them and upload to Canvas.

If handwriting or drawing is difficult for you, type your answers in the worksheet built into each walkthrough and save the PDF instead. Both routes are accepted.

Part 1. The nervous system and the neuron

Do this after the neurons and neuroglia walkthrough.

1. Draw the map of the nervous system

Start with the central nervous system at the top. Branch off the sensory division and the efferent division, split the efferent division into somatic motor and autonomic, split autonomic into sympathetic and parasympathetic, and add the enteric nervous system. Put an arrow on each branch showing which way information travels.

Under each efferent branch, write one target it controls.

2. Draw a multipolar neuron and label its regions

Draw it from memory. Label the dendrites, the cell body, the axon hillock and initial segment that make up the trigger zone, the axon, and the axon terminals. Then draw an arrow showing which way a signal travels through the cell.

Above each region, write the kind of signal it carries: graded, all or none, or chemical.

3. Write what happens at each stop

Fill in one sentence for each region, in the order a signal passes through them.

4. Three shapes

3. Traffic on the axon

Draw a long neuron with the cell body on the left and the terminals on the right, with a microtubule track running down the axon. Draw a package moving outward and one moving back, and label the motor protein that carries each. Add the slow stream as a third, much slower package.

Beside each package, write what it carries and roughly how fast it moves.

Then answer: how does a virus that enters at a nerve terminal reach the cell body, and which motor protein carries it?

Part 3. The resting membrane potential

Do this after the resting membrane potential walkthrough.

1. Set up the cell

The membrane is drawn for you. Put potassium, sodium, and the large negative proteins where they actually sit in a resting cell, using more symbols where there is more of that ion. Draw in the potassium leak channels and the few sodium channels. Mark the inside and the outside.

Write the approximate concentration of potassium and sodium on each side, with units.

2. Draw the two forces on potassium

Pick one potassium ion sitting in a leak channel. Draw an arrow for the chemical force on it and a different arrow for the electrical force. Label which is which, and mark which arrow is winning at rest.

3. Explain the number

A resting neuron reads about negative seventy millivolts. In your own words, say why it is negative, why it is closer to potassium's balance point than to sodium's, and what the pump is and is not doing.

3. Read a patient

A patient's blood potassium is 7.0 mmol/L (normal range 3.5 to 5.0 mmol/L). Explain, in the order the events happen, why this makes excitable tissue stop responding rather than fire more. Use the three channel states in your answer.
