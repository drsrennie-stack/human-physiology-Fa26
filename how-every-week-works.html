Eight steps, every week, in this order

The topic changes every week. The procedure does not. Once you have done it once you already know how to do every week after it, which means you can spend your attention on the physiology instead of on figuring out what I want.

The eight steps are the same in Canvas and on this website, under the same names. Work wherever you like. Anything you turn in goes through Canvas either way.

Do them in order. Each one is set up by the one before it. Skipping ahead to the graded pieces is the single most common way a week goes badly, because you end up applying material you have not yet gotten into your head.

Steps 1 to 3: get it in

1. Your first pass

Print the week first: the note sheet, the competency list, and the written notes. Then read, and fill in each box on the note sheet as you go, in one color. Use the book or the written notes, whichever you prefer. If you would rather take your own handwritten notes and drawings instead of the printed sheet, that is fine, as long as what you produce covers the competencies.

Working on paper matters here. Switching windows to read and type is the part that quietly eats your evening.

2. Watch the concept videos, in a second color

Switch colors and do not start a fresh sheet. Add what each video gives you that the reading did not, in the same boxes you already filled. At the end, the amount of second color on the page shows you exactly where your reading was thin, and that is what you study first.

Leave the gaps where they are. A thin box is not a mistake, it is the sheet telling you where to go back.

3. Upload your note sheet

Photograph every page with both colors on it and upload it as one file. I am not grading the sheet. I mark it complete or not complete and I read enough of it to see how the week went for you. Turning it in every week is how you show you are participating.

Graded. Complete or not complete, for participation.

Step 4: get it back out

4. Study it for several days

Now the note sheet closes and the material has to come back out of your head without looking. This is the step that decides your exam score, and it is the one nobody is checking on, which is exactly why it is worth protecting.

Four short sessions across several days beat one long night. The forgetting in between is what makes the memory stick, so the gap is doing real work even though it feels like you are losing ground.

Four ways to do it, and you should use more than one:

  • Rx Cards. Spaced recall cards for the week. The ones you miss come back tomorrow, the ones you know come back later.
  • Brain Dump. Spin a prompt, draw or write it on paper with nothing open, then tick off what you left out.
  • Book problems. Work them forward before you look, then backward from the answer to see how the author got there.
  • Study With Me. Say it out loud to another person. Teaching it is the fastest way to find what is still missing.

Not graded, and the step that decides your exam score. Nothing here goes in the gradebook. The point is to find out what you cannot do yet while it is still cheap to find out.

Steps 5 to 7: use it

5. The lab

A lab every week, either a simulation or a worked investigation. Work the lab page top to bottom, record what it asks for as you go, and turn it in as one file. It is built to be worked in order, not skimmed.

Graded. Investigation category.

6. Your patient

You keep one patient chart all term, and every week you add one entry to it. Draw that week's mechanism by hand, answer the written questions on the case page, log any AI you used and what you used it for, then add the entry to the chart behind the ones before it and upload it.

The drawing is the part that matters. Drawing a mechanism from memory is not something you can do without understanding it, which is why it is in here every single week.

Graded. Application category.

7. The discussion

Make your prediction before you look anything up, then say what actually happened and what surprised you. Posts come first, replies to classmates come after, and they have separate deadlines so there is something on the board for people to reply to.

Graded. Thinking category.

Step 8: check it

8. Mastery Check, and upload your report

Thirty questions on that week's competencies, nothing open. It names the competencies that cost you points, so you finish the week knowing what you actually have and what you only thought you had.

Take it once or take it ten times. The score is for you, not for a grade. Upload the report when you are done with it.

If the check says a competency is not solid yet, go back to Step 4 for that one competency. Not the whole week, just the piece that came up short.

Graded. Complete or not complete.

Deadlines, and what to do when a week gets away from you

Everything in a week is due Sunday night unless the week page says otherwise. Discussions are the exception, because the first post is due earlier in the week so that replies have something to reply to.

Weeks 2 and 3 are one block. You have two weeks on one set of material, and everything in the block is due Sunday, September 27. Week 4 opens Monday, September 28.

If you fall behind, do not skip Steps 1 through 4 to catch up on the graded pieces. That trade feels efficient and it is the one that costs you the exam. Tell me in the Virtual Office instead and we will work out what to do.
