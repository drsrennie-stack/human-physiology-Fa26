What this is

Your virtual lab for Week 1, run through PhysioEx. You generate real physiological output, a trace, a curve, a set of values or a calculation, and then you interpret what you got. The lab is where the mechanism from lecture stops being a diagram and starts being data.

Due Sunday at 11:59 pm, the Sunday that closes Week 1. Up to 24 hours late is an automatic 50 percent reduction. After 24 hours it is a zero.

Do it in this order

  1. Read the lab before you open itSkim the activity in the lab manual so you know what you are measuring and why. Going in cold turns a 40 minute lab into a two hour one.
  2. Predict before you run itWrite down what you expect to happen and why, in one or two lines, before you touch anything. You are not graded on being right. You are graded on having made a real prediction.
  3. Run the activityWork through it in order. Do not skip the setup screens, they usually contain the variable you will be asked about.
  4. Record as you goFill in your data record while the numbers are in front of you rather than reconstructing it afterwards from memory. Reconstructed data records are obvious and they are usually wrong.
  5. Interpret itSay what the data shows, what mechanism explains it, and whether your prediction held. If it did not, say what you were assuming that made it look right.
  6. Export and submit the PDFSave your completed report and upload it here.

What a complete data record has

  • Your prediction, written before you ran the activity.
  • Every value the activity asked you to record, with units. A number without a unit is not a result.
  • The trace, curve or table the activity produced.
  • Your interpretation: what happened, and the mechanism that explains it.
  • One line on whether your prediction held, and what you would change about your reasoning.

Using AI on this

AI is open here, and it is genuinely useful for checking whether your interpretation holds together. Use it to argue with, not to answer for you. The prediction and the interpretation have to be your own reasoning, and you should be able to talk through every line of your report without notes, because the midterm will ask you to.
