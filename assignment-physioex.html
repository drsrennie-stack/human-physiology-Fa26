What this is

PhysioEx is where you generate the data. The lab report is what you turn in, and it is built entirely around the numbers you got on your screen. Nobody else has your run, which is what makes this yours.

It is written and drawn by hand, on one page, and it takes about thirty minutes once the activity is done. It is not a write-up of what the textbook says should happen. It is an account of what actually happened when you ran it.

Due Sunday at 11:59 pm, the Sunday that closes Week 1. Up to 24 hours late is an automatic 50 percent reduction. After 24 hours it is a zero.

Do it in this order

  1. Write your prediction first, before you open PhysioExOne or two sentences: what do you expect to happen, and what mechanism are you predicting from? Date it. This has to be on the page before you have seen any data, and it is the part I read most carefully.
  2. Run the activity in PhysioExWork through it in order. Do not skip the setup screens, they usually hold the variable you will be asked about later.
  3. Copy your own numbers onto the page by handEvery value the activity produced, with units. Transcribe them yourself rather than pasting a screenshot. Writing a number down is when you notice it is strange.
  4. Draw your own graph or traceBy hand, axes labelled, units on both. Draw what your run produced, not the clean version from the book. If yours came out messy, draw it messy.
  5. Reconcile it with your predictionDid it hold? If not, what were you assuming that made it look right? This is worth more than being right was.
  6. Answer the perturbation questionEach week I change one condition and ask what your result would become. You answer from your own numbers, not from the general principle.
  7. Record the sixty second explanationHold up your drawn graph and talk me through it: what you did, what you got, and why it came out that way. One take, no script, no notes.

Why this one cannot be done with AI

Read this part

AI has never seen your data. It does not know what your run produced, what your prediction was before you ran it, or what your graph looks like. Every part of this report depends on one of those three things, which is why the assignment is built this way rather than as a set of questions about the material.

You are welcome to use AI while you are learning the physiology behind the activity, and it is good for arguing with when you are stuck on a mechanism. What it cannot do is produce the artifact: your handwriting, your drawing, your numbers, your voice.

A report that reads like a textbook explanation of what should happen, rather than an account of what did happen, is the thing I notice fastest. If your data disagrees with the book, say so and try to explain why. That is a better report than one where everything came out perfect.

How to submit it

  • Photograph the page in colour, in good light. Hold your phone flat above it so the axes stay straight and the writing stays readable. Check the image before you upload rather than after.
  • Upload the photo and the video to this assignment. Canvas Studio is fine for the video, so is a phone recording.
  • Keep the original page. All fifteen of them together are the best midterm study set you will have, because they are in your own hand.
