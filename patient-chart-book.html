Your patient chart, all term.

Two patients and two charts that you keep by hand, one patient at a time. Camila Reyes runs from Week 1 to Week 8, and until Midterm 1 she is the only one you work on; her chart and analysis are turned in on Sunday, November 1. Dale Whitcomb runs from Week 9 to Week 15, and his chart and analysis are turned in on Wednesday, December 16. Print the book once. Every week, copy that week's released results into the flowsheets, add to the problem list, draw the control loop on that week's page, and answer the questions. By December you will be able to run a finger along one row and read a whole admission.

How to use it

  1. Print the whole book once, in September, and put it in a binder. Do not reprint it.
  2. Each week, open this week's entry on screen. It gives you the released results.
  3. Copy the numbers into the flowsheets first. That transcription is where you notice a trend. Do not skip it.
  4. Then fill in that week's page: what changed, the problem list, the drawing, the five questions, and your entry point's question.
  5. Keep the page in your binder. Nothing is turned in weekly; each patient's pages come in together when that file closes.

Why on paper, and why two charts

  • A chart on a ward grows. It is not rewritten each week, and neither is this one.
  • Trends live in columns. A number you type into a box and never see again cannot show you a trend.
  • Camila has one thing wrong at a time, so you can see a single system clearly. Dale has four at once, which is what Weeks 9 to 15 are about.
  • Nothing on this page is saved. Write on it.

Reading a results table

  • Every released result carries its units and its reference range, the way a hospital result screen does.
  • A value outside the range is flagged in words, not in color, so it survives photocopying and reaches a screen reader.
  • A reference range is not a health range. Week 1 asks you what it is a range of.
