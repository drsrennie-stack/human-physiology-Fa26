How to use it

  1. Print the whole chart once, about twenty pages, and keep it in a folder. All portrait, so any printer will do.
  2. Each week, open that week's chart entry on the site, then write the new numbers into the flowsheets in the column for that week. Dates are on the column heads.
  3. On that week's page, draw the control loop, answer the five questions, then the one from your entry point.
  4. Keep it. Nothing from the chart is turned in week to week. The chart is your capstone, worth 5 percent of the course. You turn in the whole thing once, as one PDF, on Wednesday, December 16 (exactly what to upload), and it is graded as one piece: the trends, the problem list, the loops, and your thinking across all fifteen weeks. What you do turn in each Sunday is that week's Use It case.

Why on paper, and why one chart

  • Nobody on a ward reads one day of a chart. The trend is the finding. Fifteen columns side by side is the point.
  • Writing a number into a column next to last week's number makes you compare them. Typing it into a box does not.
  • Week 1 is already filled in as the baseline column, because you have done it. Everything from Week 2 on gets compared to that column.
  • Your entry point (nursing, medicine, radiology, respiratory therapy, exercise) does not change the chart. It changes the last question on each week's page.
