How a graded potential is made

  1. A stimulus opens gated channels: a neurotransmitter opens ligand-gated channels at a synapse, or stretch opens mechanically gated channels in a sensory ending.
  2. Ions cross, and the membrane moves toward the equilibrium potential of the ion whose permeability went up.
  3. Sodium in: the inside becomes less negative, a depolarizing graded potential.
  4. Potassium out: the inside becomes more negative, a hyperpolarizing graded potential, which moves the cell away from firing.
  5. The channels close and the membrane returns to rest. A graded potential lasts from a few milliseconds to tens of milliseconds.
  • A stronger stimulus opens more channels or holds them open longer, so more ions cross and the change is larger. That is what graded means.
  • Example sizes recorded at one spot: weak stimulus 3 mV, medium 7 mV, strong 12 mV; a potassium-opening stimulus 8 mV below rest.

Why a graded potential fades as it spreads

  • Local current flow: the positive charge that enters spreads along the inside of the dendrite in both directions.
  • Current leak: some of that charge escapes through the open leak channels along the membrane, like water from a leaky hose.
  • Cytoplasmic resistance: the cytoplasm resists the flow of charge, the way a thin wire resists current more than a thick one.
  • Together they make the change smaller at every point farther from the channels that opened. Example: 20 mV at point 1 next to the channels, then 12, 7 and 4 mV at points farther along.
  • A wider dendrite has lower cytoplasmic resistance, so a graded potential spreads farther. Current leak does not change, because it depends on the leak channels in the membrane.
Local current flow
Spread of charge through the cytoplasm away from where ions entered.
Current leak
Loss of charge across the membrane through open leak channels.
Cytoplasmic resistance
The cytoplasm's resistance to the flow of charge; lower in a wider process.

What reaches the trigger zone

  • The trigger zone fires only if the membrane there reaches threshold, about −55 mV. From rest at −70 mV that takes about 15 mV of depolarization.
  • What counts is the size of the graded potential when it arrives, not where it started.
  • Graded potentials that overlap in time add together: summation. Depolarizing ones add; hyperpolarizing ones subtract.
  • Real graded potentials that overlap add to a little less than their plain sum. The reasoning is the same.

The voltage at the trigger zone, about −55 mV, at which an action potential fires.

Patient: the pufferfish meal

  • Tetrodotoxin plugs voltage-gated sodium channels from the outside. It does not block chemically or mechanically gated channels.
  • His resting potential is normal (−70 mV). Weak stimuli still make small graded potentials, about 5 mV, that fade.
  • A strong stimulus makes about 20 mV, which goes past threshold, but no action potential appears, because the channels that make the action potential are plugged.
  • His neurons can still take in signals but cannot send them down an axon. The action potential walkthrough finishes this case.
