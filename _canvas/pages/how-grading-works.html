BIO 005 Human Physiology · Start here, read once

Four categories, each named for what it asks you to do rather than for the format it arrives in. The work that carries the most weight is the work that proves the reasoning is yours.

All four sit in the Apply stage of the week, except the two midterms. Everything in Learn, Practice and Check carries no points, and the reason for that is at the bottom of this page.

The four categories

35 percent of your grade

Knowledge

Two exams, 17.5 percent each. Midterm 1 is open resource and open AI. You get two assigned cases, 50 points each. For each case you compare at least two interventions and choose one you can defend, draw a three-panel model by hand (normal, disrupted, and your intervention), check your sources and one claim an AI tool gave you, and make a visible revision to your model. Everything you turn in is handwritten and scanned into one PDF, and anything typed earns a zero for that case. Video is optional. Most of your score comes from how you reached your decision, not from the decision alone.

Midterm 1 covers Weeks 1 to 8, in a window from November 5 to 8. Midterm 2 covers Weeks 10 to 14, in a window from December 14 to 16. Each one is a window of several days rather than an hour, so you can pick your time.

25 percent of your grade

Investigation

The weekly labs. You generate and interpret real physiological output and write it up in the same structure a clinical write up uses: question, prediction, evidence, interpretation, conclusion.

Where a week uses PhysioEx, it takes both halves. PhysioEx has to show complete in Pearson, and the points live on your worksheet.

From Week 10, the labs are 15 percent and the Mastery Checks are 10 percent. Both have a target and two graded attempts.

25 percent of your grade

Application

20 percent is your weekly application case, chosen from three or four set in different rooms and turned in each Sunday. They differ in context, not in rigor, and every one of them assesses the same underlying competency.

5 percent is your patient chart, the capstone. You keep it by hand all term, adding each week's data and your thinking about it, and nothing is collected week to week. Camila’s chart and analysis were turned in on Sunday, November 8, with Midterm 1.

From Week 10, cases are 15 percent and lecture drawings are 10 percent: two cases on each week’s topic in the midterm format, and one drawing for each lecture. There is no patient chart in Part 2; the cases take its place.

15 percent of your grade

Thinking

Through Week 9, one discussion post a week, carrying the physiology and your thinking about it together: the decision you made, the evidence, and what you adjusted. The initial post is due Friday at 10:00 pm Pacific and replies Sunday at 10:00 pm Pacific.

From Week 10, a few projects take the place of the discussion, such as the Wiggers diagram, the GI map and the renal map.

From Week 10

The mastery pathway

On every lecture drawing, case, lab, project and Mastery Check you pick a target, 70, 80, 90 or 100 percent, and your score is the highest level your work shows. Each one has one revision. What is not graded, and why that is deliberate

Several things you do every week carry no points at all.

  • The Competency Study Guide and the retrieval work. This is where you find out what you do not know. Grading it would push you to make it look finished instead of honest, and an honest sheet with gaps in it is worth more to you than a tidy one.
  • The practice items. Predict, commit, check, correct, explain. Getting these wrong is the point of doing them.
  • The book problems. Work them forwards if you have a way in. If you open one and have no idea how to start, read the worked solution and write beside each line why that step is there, then reproduce it from blank paper.
  • The Mastery Check. No points and no penalty, and you can take it as many times as you want. It does score you, and it does more than that: it reports competency by competency, separates what you got wrong while feeling sure from what you got wrong while unsure, and tells you what to go back to. Through Week 9, none of those numbers reach the gradebook. From Week 10 the Mastery Check is graded: you pick a target and get two graded attempts. The randomized practice exam stays ungraded.
  • Spaced recall. Week 3 has to still be there in October, and spacing is what does that.

None of this is optional in any way that matters. It is the whole route to the four categories above.

The two things you turn in that carry no points

Your Competency Study Guide, after both passes, and your Mastery Check report. Each is marked complete or not complete, never scored. They are how I see that you are working through the week, and how I know when to reach out. A student who stops turning them in is not participating in the class, and continued participation is a condition of staying enrolled.

Late work

Up to 24 hours late loses half the credit. After 24 hours it is a zero. You have the whole week and you choose your own hours, so plan for the emergency rather than around it.
