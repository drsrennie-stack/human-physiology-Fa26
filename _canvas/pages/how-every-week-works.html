BIO 005 Human Physiology · Read once, use every week

Every week has the same four stages

Learn, Practice, Apply, Check. Every week of this course runs through those four in that order, and each stage holds a few steps. You learn the shape once, in Week 1, and after that you spend your attention on the physiology instead of on finding things.

Each step is its own page in Canvas, with one job on it and the buttons for that job. The Next button at the bottom takes you to the following step, so you are never deciding what to do next.

Three things you should be able to answer in five seconds anywhere in the course: where am I, what do I do next, and what is due. The stage label at the top of every page answers the first, the Next button answers the second, and the week overview answers the third.

The four stages, and the steps inside them

Learn

Learn it with Dr. Rennie

Read first with your Competency Study Guide open, then watch me teach it, then go back and add what changed.

  • Step 1. Print your week Competency list, Competency Study Guide, brain dump paper. Printing is optional; ruling the boxes onto your own paper is treated exactly the same.
  • Step 2. First pass, in your first color Read the book chapters or my notes and fill in the Competency Study Guide boxes with drawings, labels and short lists. Try the competency prompts. Leave the gaps.
  • Step 3. Second pass, in your second color Watch the week's videos in order and add what they gave you that the reading did not. The second color shows you exactly where your reading was thin.
  • Step 4. Upload your Competency Study Guide Photograph or scan the sheet with both colors on it and upload it. Marked complete or not complete, never graded for a score. It is how you show you are participating.

Practice

Try it from memory

Get it back without looking. Brain dumps, drawing, recall cards, book problems. Mistakes here are useful and none of it is graded.

  • Step 5. Study it for several days About an hour a day: recall cards, draw it from memory, brain dumps, book problems, Study With Me. Spacing is the whole trick, because the forgetting in between is what makes it stick.

Apply

Use what you learned

The lab, the application case and the discussion, all graded. Plus your patient chart, which you keep all term and turn in once in December.

  • Step 6. Lab The week's lab, with a sheet you fill in by hand and turn in. Graded, Investigation.
  • Step 7. Your patient One entry in your patient's chart each week: draw the loop, answer the questions, log your AI use. Graded, Application.
  • Step 8. Discussion One post carrying some physiology from the week and what you learned about your own thinking. Post Friday, replies Sunday. Graded, Thinking.

Check

Find the gaps

A thirty question check on this week's competencies. Nothing here is graded. A low score tells you exactly what to go back to.

  • Step 9. Mastery Check, and upload your report A practice exam on the week with nothing open, then upload the report. Complete or not complete. A low score is information, not a grade: it names the competencies to go back to.

Why two colors

The Competency Study Guide is one big box per competency, and you fill each box twice. The first pass, from the book and the notes, is done before you watch anything. That pass is your baseline: what your own reading produced. The second pass, from the videos, goes into the same box in a different color. When you are done, the second color is a map of what the reading did not give you, and that map is what you study from.

Do it in this order. A student who watches the video first recognizes the material instead of producing it, and the sheet stops showing the gap that makes the week work.

Keep it to drawings, labels, arrows and short lists. If you need words, put them in small boxes with arrows between them, in the order things happen. Sentences running across the page do not count, because that is not how the exam will ask you.

When things are due

Friday, 10:00 pm Pacific: your discussion post.

Sunday, 10:00 pm Pacific: your discussion replies, the lab, your patient chart entry, your Competency Study Guide upload, and your Mastery Check report.

Every week opens on its Monday. If you have finished and submitted the current week, the next one unlocks early on Saturday at 8:00 pm Pacific so you can start over the weekend. Late work: up to 24 hours late loses half the credit, and after that it is a zero. Plan for emergencies.

Participation, and staying enrolled

Two things are turned in every week that carry no points: your Competency Study Guide, after both passes, and your Mastery Check report. Each is marked complete or not complete. They are how I see that you are working through the week, and how I know when to reach out. A student who stops turning them in is not participating in the class, and continued participation is a condition of staying enrolled. This is not a hoop. The sheet is the work, and uploading it takes two minutes.

What is graded

  • Knowledge, 35 percent. Two draw and teach midterms.
  • Investigation, 25 percent. The weekly lab.
  • Application, 25 percent. The weekly patient case and your chart.
  • Thinking, 15 percent. The weekly discussion.

All of the graded work sits in the Apply stage, plus the two midterms. Learn, Practice and Check carry no points on purpose, so you can do them the way that works for your brain. The Competency Study Guide upload and the Mastery Check report are tracked complete or not complete because they show me you are in the course.
