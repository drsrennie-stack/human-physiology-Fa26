Step 1. Pre-read, before anything else

Time: 30 minutes at most. Counts for: Turned in. Complete or not complete.

A quick look ahead, not a full read. You look at the headings and figures and answer a few short questions, so the walkthroughs make more sense when you get to them.

What to do

  1. Choose how you will do the pre-read, in the Choose one box below: on screen, or on paper.
  2. If you have the Silverthorn textbook, read these parts closely. In Chapter 13, read the section on skeletal muscle: how a muscle is built, how a nerve signal makes it contract, where its energy comes from, and how it tires. Then read the section on the mechanics of body movement. In Chapter 12, read only the part on the somatic motor division, where a motor neuron meets a muscle fiber. In Chapter 14, read the section on how the brain controls body movement, the part after the reflexes you read last week. Skip smooth and cardiac muscle for now; they come later, with the heart.
  3. If you are using the free OpenStax book instead, read these six sections. 10.2 shows how a skeletal muscle is built. 10.3 explains how the filaments slide and what calcium does. 10.4 covers twitches, summation and motor units. 10.5 compares the kinds of muscle fiber. 10.6 covers what exercise and disuse do to a muscle. 14.3 covers how the brain and spinal cord command a movement.
  4. About the figures, if you use OpenStax. The pre-read names each figure by what it shows. Look for a picture of the same thing in those OpenStax sections. If you cannot find a matching picture, write your own list instead: on a sheet of paper, list each picture you did find in those sections and write one sentence about what it shows. Upload that page with your pre-read.
  5. Answer the questions on the pre-read in a few words each. Short answers are fine.
  6. Save the page as a PDF with the button at the bottom, or photograph your paper copy.

Chapter numbers are for the 9th edition. Other editions number things differently, so go by the chapter title and the topic, and use the search in your eText.
