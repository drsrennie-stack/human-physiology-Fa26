The goal is to learn where things are in the book so you can find them later when you have a question or want more examples. You can complete the pre-read online and save it as a PDF, or print it and fill it in by hand. First pass and second pass

On your first pass, use everything: the walkthroughs, the videos, your notes, and your reading. Then study for a few days with the recall cards and brain dumps, and go back for a second pass on the areas you struggled with. Do your second pass in a different color so I can see what you went back to. Please label everything on your Competency Study Guide, and wait until you have finished your second pass before you upload it.

Recall cards and brain dumps

Recall cards run in 20-card sessions and bring back the cards you missed on a later day. The brain dump tool is already set to Week 4 and gives you a random prompt to draw on paper. Try to build your answer before you look at anything, even if you have to look things up in your book, notes, or videos first. When you are done, use the checklist to check your work. You can also use the Copy for ChatGPT button and upload a photo of your drawing to get feedback on what you covered and what you missed.

Mastery check

This week you need at least three mastery check attempts, spread over different days, with studying in between. Upload the reports from all of your attempts. The mastery check does not count for points. The questions increase in difficulty and are designed to find your gaps, so I expect first attempts to be well below perfect. What I am looking for is improvement from one attempt to the next. Please do not use AI to answer these questions; it takes away the practice that the mastery check is meant to give you.

Patient chart

Your case and patient chart are now combined, and the chart is electronic, similar to what you would use in a hospital. You will keep the same patient until the midterm, and you will turn in the whole chart in Week 8, so there is nothing to upload for the chart this week. Starting in Week 9, you will get a new, critically ill patient that lets us look at every body system. You are not diagnosing anyone. You are learning the physiology behind the patient data.

What is graded this week

  • For points: the Exam Practice (the two practice questions and the discussion) and the PhysioEx Lab (Exercise 2, nine activities, one point each).
  • Complete or incomplete: everything else. These items show 0 points in Canvas.

Your final grade is based on the percentage you earn in each grading category, not on total points, so the number of points on an assignment does not change how much it counts. The gradebook is set up this way now, so your current grade should be close to what it will look like at the end of the semester.

Your first practice exam

This week you will practice the exact format of your exams, so that by exam day the process feels routine and nothing goes wrong with the technology.

  1. Test your recording first. Before you start, make a short test recording with Canvas Studio so you know where it is and how your camera works. When you open a question, look in the answer box for the Studio icon that looks like a small TV. There is a picture of it in the directions.
  2. Two questions, taken separately. Each practice question is its own Canvas quiz, and each one is timed at about 25 minutes.
  3. Build first (15 minutes). Read the multiple-choice question and start recording. Spend up to 15 minutes building a model on your whiteboard that answers the question. This can be a mind map, a drawing, or any model that shows the physiology. Build quietly first; do not teach while you build.
  4. Then teach (5 to 7 minutes). Explain why the correct answer is correct and where each wrong choice breaks down, using your model. Stay within 5 to 7 minutes, because the timing is part of the grade.
  5. Give your answer. State the letter of the answer you chose.

The letter answer is worth 25 percent of each question. The other 75 percent is your model and how clearly you explain it. I am not grading how well you speak. If you misspeak, correct yourself right there and you will not lose points for it. What counts is the physiology.

If you have accommodations, your extra time is added to your building time, not your teaching time. I will set that up for you, so you do not need to do anything.

After both questions, go to the discussion, answer the questions about how the practice went, and reply to two classmates with something useful. Next week I will share the answer key and the rubric I use for grading. You will rewatch your own videos, check them against the rubric, and write about what you got right, what you missed, and what surprised you.

The midterm uses this same setup. You will have the exam window to complete it, and you can spread the questions out across different days instead of doing them all at once. Please do not wait until the last minute.

Plan your week

This class takes about 18 to 20 hours a week, and more if biology or anatomy is new to you. It cannot be done in one weekend. Set a weekly schedule, follow the steps in order, and reach out early if something is not working. I cannot be much help in the last hour before something is due, so ask your questions early in the week.
