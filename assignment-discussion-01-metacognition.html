Week 1 is the only week with a Sunday post date. The vision board is already due Friday, and three of these questions are about a Mastery Check you take later in the week. From Week 2 on: post by Friday, two replies by Sunday.

This week you chose how to learn the material. Your Mastery Check then told you whether that choice worked, before any grade depended on it. This post is where you look at that evidence and say what you did with it: decision, evidence, adjustment, the same three moves as a clinical write up, turned on yourself.

Answer these four

The four questions, with what counts as an answer
QuestionWhat countsWhat does not
1. What did your Mastery Check reveal?One specific thing you thought you knew and did not. "I could name the three parts of a control loop but kept calling the integrating center a receptor.""Feedback loops."
2. What did you do about it?Changed the resource, the approach, drew it, said it out loud, asked someone. Or kept what you were doing, if you can say how you knew it was working."I studied more."
3. What happened when you tried again?Whether it held, and how you could tell. "I rebuilt the loop from a blank page without looking.""It felt better."
4. The physiology questionWhat you thought homeostasis meant before this week, and what you think it means now. If it has not moved, say that, and say what would move it.A textbook definition copied in.

Questions 1 to 3 are the same all fifteen weeks. Only question 4 changes.

Your numbers are yours

You do not have to post your Mastery Check or practice exam results. The score, the count, the number of attempts can all stay private. "Improving a lot," "improving a little," "holding steady," or "sliding" tells your classmates everything useful. Share the numbers too if you want to; that is your call.

Your practice log goes to me separately, as an assignment. That one is between us, so I can reach out if I see you struggling.

How to post

  • Where: the Week 1 Reflect discussion in Canvas.
  • Length: four short paragraphs, one per question, one screen. Specific beats long.
  • Format: text, or a two minute video of you answering the four questions.
  • Order: take your Mastery Check first. Three of the four questions are about it.
  • Bad week? Say so and answer anyway. "I ran out of time, skipped the retrieval, and the check showed me what that cost" is a strong post. You are graded on whether you looked at what happened and said something true about it, not on whether the week went well.

Replies, if you write one

Optional this week, required from Week 2. Read a few posts anyway; how other people are studying is most of the value of the thread.

  • Take their adjustment and ask what happens if they try it on something else.
  • Name where their reasoning and yours came apart, and ask about it rather than correcting it.
  • Offer what worked for you on the same gap, specifically enough to do tomorrow.
  • Ask the question their post left open.

"Great post, I struggled with that too" is kind and does not count. Add the sentence that comes after it.
