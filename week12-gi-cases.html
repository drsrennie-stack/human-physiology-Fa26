How it works

One test, one patient

  1. Claim a case below. Your patient and their results appear.
  2. Learn the physiology the test or treatment depends on (the list tells you what to look up).
  3. Draw by hand: where in the gut it looks or acts, what normal shows, what your patient shows, and the physiology behind the difference.
  4. Explain it in writing. Then get your twist and add what it changes.

    Your twist

    What each level needs

    Case levels
    LevelWhat your work has to show
    70%FoundationalYour case claimed. The four-part drawing (where, normal, your patient, the physiology), with your patient’s numbers on it. Beside it, write the notes section, video or textbook part it comes from.
    80%MechanisticExplain the difference between normal and your patient with cause and effect, naming the cells, secretions, hormones or reflexes involved.
    90%AppliedYour twist added and explained. Answer the “point to it” prompt from your drawing. Name one claim from a source you used (an AI tool, a website or a video) and how you checked it.
    100%IntegratedBring in a second system (for example the liver, the pancreas, the immune system or the nervous system) and explain how it changes your patient’s result or recovery. Find one thing your drawing makes too simple, fix it, and mark the change.

    Each level includes everything in the levels above it. Your score is the highest level your work shows, even if you aimed lower.

    Step 4

    • Write your target, 70, 80, 90 or 100 percent, at the top of your first page.
    • Everything is handwritten on paper. Cross out mistakes instead of erasing them, and leave them visible.
    • Photograph your pages twice: once when the drawing is about half done, and once when it is finished. Both photos go in.
    • Upload one PDF in Canvas by Sunday, November 29 at 10:00 pm, and post your case in the Week 12 discussion.
    • You have one revision. If you revise, mark what you changed.
