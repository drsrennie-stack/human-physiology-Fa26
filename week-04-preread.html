Week 4 pre-read

About 30 minutes. Graded complete or incomplete. Do this before the first pass.

This is a quick look ahead, not a full read. Look at the headings and the figures in both chapters, answer the questions below in a few words, and move on. You will learn all of it properly in the slides, and it will make much more sense because you have already seen it once.

Where to look

First, Silverthorn Chapter 5, Membrane Dynamics: the part near the end of the chapter on the resting membrane potential. You read the rest of Chapter 5 in Week 2, so this is the one piece you have not seen yet.

Then, Silverthorn Chapter 9, Neurons: Cellular and Network Properties. Skim the whole chapter. Read the headings and look at the figures, especially the ones named below.

Chapter numbers are for the 9th edition. Other editions number things differently, so go by the chapter title and the topic, and use the search in your eText to find it.

What this reading should help you do

Read each goal and pick the answer that fits you right now. There is no wrong answer here.

The resting membrane potential (Chapter 5)

Explain what it means for a cell to have a resting membrane potential.
Describe which ions create the resting membrane potential, and why.
Explain how changing the membrane's permeability to an ion changes the membrane potential, with examples.

Neurons (Chapter 9)

Map how the nervous system is organized.
Name the parts of a neuron and a synapse and what each part does, and name the types of glial cells.
Explain how graded potentials and action potentials differ, which ions move during an action potential, what the refractory periods are, and how myelin speeds up conduction.
Tell electrical synapses from chemical synapses, and describe how neurotransmitters are made, released, and stopped.
Explain how a neuron adds up the excitatory and inhibitory signals it receives, and how a synapse can get stronger with use.
